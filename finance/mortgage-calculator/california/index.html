Property taxes and insurance in California

Under Proposition 13, California’s base property tax is 1% of a home’s assessed value, and the assessed value can rise by at most 2% a year until the home is sold. Voter-approved local bonds and assessments add to that, which is why many bills land between about 1.1% and 1.25% of the purchase price in the first year.

Because a home is reassessed at its purchase price when it sells, new buyers often pay far more than long-time neighbors in identical houses. Budget from the price you pay, not the current owner’s tax bill.

Home insurance has become harder to find in wildfire-prone areas. Get quotes early; if private insurers decline, the California FAIR Plan is the insurer of last resort, usually paired with a separate policy for other coverage.

The median owner-occupied home in Los Angeles County is worth $834,200, and the median property tax bill is $5,675 a year, an effective rate of 0.67%. Here’s the monthly payment at 7.28% for 30 years with California’s average insurance of $1,492 a year:

California compared with the national average

California’s average homeowners insurance premium of $1,492 a year is $77 less than the US average of $1,569 (NAIC, 2022), or about $6 a month less on your payment. Property tax rates vary too much by county for a single national comparison; Los Angeles County’s 0.67% rate means about $466 a month on a median home.

Frequently asked questions

What is the property tax rate in California?

It depends on the county and city. In Los Angeles County, the state’s most populous county, the effective rate on owner-occupied homes is 0.67% (Tax Foundation, 2024 Census data), with a median bill of $5,675 a year on a median home worth $834,200.

How much is homeowners insurance in California?

The average HO-3 homeowners policy in California cost $1,492 a year in 2022, according to the NAIC, compared with $1,569 nationally. Your premium depends on the home’s value, age, location and coverage.

What is the monthly payment on a median-priced home in Los Angeles County?

With 20% down at 7.28% for 30 years, a $834,200 home costs about $4,566 a month in principal and interest, plus $466 property tax and $124 insurance, about $5,156 in total.

Do California mortgage rates differ from the national average?

Rates vary more by borrower than by state: credit score, down payment, loan type and points matter most. This calculator starts with the national Freddie Mac average (7.28% as of Nov 19, 2026); replace it with a quote from a California lender.

How much should I budget beyond the mortgage payment in California?

Plan for closing costs at purchase, often 2% to 5% of the price, plus maintenance of around 1% of the home’s value a year. Because a home is reassessed at its purchase price when it sells, new buyers often pay far more than long-time neighbors in identical houses.
