On a $75,000 salary in Ohio, a single filer paid every two weeks with 5% to a 401(k) takes home about $2,210.91 per paycheck, with $1,184 a year in Ohio state taxes.

What this means

Out of $75,000 in gross pay, you keep $57,484 a year ($2,210.91 per paycheck) after taxes and deductions.

Your federal taxable income is $55,150 after the $16,100 standard deduction and pre-tax contributions, which puts you in the 22% bracket. Only income above that bracket's threshold is taxed at 22%.

Social Security and Medicare take $5,738 a year: 7.65% of wages (6.2% + 1.45%).

Your $3,750 401(k) contribution goes into your retirement account, not to the IRS, and lowers this year’s federal income tax.

How Ohio taxes your paycheck

From 2026 Ohio taxes income at a flat 2.75% above $26,050, with the first $26,050 untaxed, and most cities add their own income tax.

Ohio’s 2026 change replaced its brackets with a single 2.75% rate above $26,050.

The personal exemption depends on income: $2,400 per person up to $40,000, $2,150 up to $80,000 and $1,900 above that, with none at $500,000 or more. These are the 2025 amounts, carried into 2026 because inflation indexing is frozen.

Local income taxes

  • Most cities and villages: Ohio municipalities levy their own income taxes, commonly 1% to 2.5% (Columbus and Cleveland charge 2.5%). Enter your city’s rate under More options.

Frequently asked questions

Does Ohio have a state income tax?

From 2026 Ohio taxes income at a flat 2.75% above $26,050, with the first $26,050 untaxed, and most cities add their own income tax.

How much is taken out of a paycheck in Ohio?

On a $75,000 salary, a single filer paid biweekly with 5% to a 401(k) pays about $6,845 in federal income tax, $5,738 in Social Security and Medicare, and $1,184 in Ohio taxes a year, keeping $57,484.

What is the take-home pay on $100,000 in Ohio?

A single filer earning $100,000 in Ohio, with no 401(k) or other deductions, takes home about $77,199 a year, or $2,969.18 every two weeks.

Are there local income taxes in Ohio?

Most cities and villages: Ohio municipalities levy their own income taxes, commonly 1% to 2.5% (Columbus and Cleveland charge 2.5%). Enter your city’s rate under More options.

How accurate is this Ohio paycheck estimate?

It uses 2026 federal brackets and Ohio’s 2026 tax rules for a full year of pay. Actual paychecks can differ because of W-4 and state withholding choices, bonuses, benefits and mid-year changes. Compare it with your pay stub.
