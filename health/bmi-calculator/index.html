How to use this BMI calculator

Choose your units, enter your height and weight, and your BMI appears instantly with your weight category and the healthy weight range for your height. Switching between pounds and kilograms converts what you’ve entered, so you don’t need to retype it.

  1. Units. Pick lb, ft/in for US units or kg, cm for metric.
  2. Height. Feet and inches, or centimeters. Measure without shoes, standing straight.
  3. Weight. A morning weight before eating is the most consistent.

This calculator is for adults aged 20 and over. For children and teens, BMI is judged against growth charts for their age and sex instead.

Worked example: 5 feet 7 inches and 182 pounds

Someone who is 5 feet 7 inches (67 inches) tall and weighs 182 pounds:

  • BMI: 703 × 182 ÷ 67² = 28.5
  • Category: overweight (25.0 to 29.9)
  • Healthy weight for 5 ft 7 in: about 118 to 159 pounds (BMI 18.5 to 24.9)
  • Distance to the healthy range: about 23 pounds above the upper bound

In metric terms, a person who is 160 cm tall and weighs 52 kg has a BMI of 52 ÷ 1.60² = 20.3, in the healthy weight range of 47.4 to 63.7 kg for that height.

Notice how wide the healthy range is: about 40 pounds at 5 feet 7 inches. BMI isn’t meant to pick one ideal weight; it flags weights that are statistically linked with higher health risk across large populations.

The BMI formula in plain English

BMI compares your weight with your height, so taller people can weigh more at the same BMI.

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)², or in US units, BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)²

The 703 converts pounds and inches into the metric result. Calcrafty converts with the exact definitions (1 pound = 0.45359237 kg and 1 inch = 2.54 cm) and reports BMI to one decimal place, as clinicians do. The Show the math panel above walks through your numbers.

To find the healthy weight range for a height, the formula is turned around: weight = BMI × height². Using BMI values of 18.5 and 24.9 gives the lower and upper healthy weights.

BMI categories for adults

BMI Category
Below 18.5 Underweight
18.5 to 24.9 Healthy weight
25.0 to 29.9 Overweight
30.0 to 34.9 Obesity, class 1
35.0 to 39.9 Obesity, class 2
40.0 and above Obesity, class 3 (sometimes called severe obesity)

These are the categories used by the CDC and the World Health Organization. Some organizations use lower thresholds for people of Asian descent, because health risks can rise at lower BMIs in those populations.

What BMI does and doesn’t tell you

BMI is popular because it’s free, quick and easy to repeat. At a population level, higher BMI is associated with higher risk of conditions such as type 2 diabetes, heart disease and high blood pressure. But for an individual it’s a rough screen:

  • It ignores body composition. Muscle weighs more than fat for its size, so strength athletes can read as overweight.
  • It ignores where fat is stored. Fat around the waist carries more health risk than fat on the hips. A waist circumference above 35 inches for women or 40 inches for men is a common warning sign.
  • It ignores age, sex and ethnicity. The same BMI can mean different amounts of body fat in different groups.

That’s why a doctor will look at BMI alongside other measures rather than on its own.

Waist-to-height ratio: a useful second check

Because BMI can’t see where fat is stored, many clinicians now pair it with a simple tape measure. The waist-to-height ratio divides your waist circumference by your height, in the same units. The UK’s National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) recommends keeping your waist to less than half your height, a ratio below 0.5, for adults with a BMI under 35.

For someone who is 5 feet 7 inches (67 inches) tall, that means a waist under 33.5 inches. To measure, wrap the tape around your middle halfway between the bottom of your ribs and the top of your hip bones, breathe out normally and read it without pulling tight. The ratio works the same way for men and women and across heights, which is part of its appeal.

BMI in specific groups

The standard adult categories are a starting point, but several groups need a different reading:

  • Athletes and very muscular people can have a BMI in the overweight range with little body fat. A body fat estimate or waist measurement gives a better picture.
  • Older adults often lose muscle with age, so a “healthy” BMI can hide more body fat than it would in a younger person. Some research suggests a BMI slightly above 25 isn’t linked with higher risk in later life, so goals are best set with a doctor.
  • People of Asian descent may face higher risk of type 2 diabetes and heart disease at lower BMIs. The World Health Organization has suggested lower action points for some Asian populations, and some US guidelines recommend diabetes screening from a BMI of 23 for Asian Americans.
  • During pregnancy, BMI isn’t used to judge current weight. Instead, your BMI before pregnancy guides how much weight gain is recommended; ask your prenatal care provider.
  • Children and teens use age- and sex-specific percentiles, as the FAQ above explains.

Changing your BMI safely

You don’t need to reach the middle of the healthy range to see benefits. According to the CDC, losing a modest 5% to 10% of body weight can improve blood pressure, cholesterol and blood sugar for people with overweight or obesity. For someone weighing 200 pounds, that’s 10 to 20 pounds.

  • Go gradually. Losing about 1 to 2 pounds a week is generally considered safe and is more likely to last than rapid weight loss.
  • Protect your muscle. Strength training two or more days a week, as the US physical activity guidelines recommend, and enough protein help make sure the weight you lose is mostly fat.
  • If your BMI is below 18.5, gaining weight slowly with nutritious, energy-dense foods is the usual approach, and it’s worth asking a doctor to rule out an underlying cause.
  • Get support for larger changes. Doctors, registered dietitians and structured programs can help, and some people with a high BMI may benefit from medication or surgery, which only a clinician can assess. Whatever the approach, regular check-ins help you adjust before small changes become large ones.

Tracking BMI over time

A single BMI reading matters less than the trend. Weight naturally moves by a few pounds from day to day with water, salt and meal timing, so compare readings taken under the same conditions: the same scale, the same time of day and similar clothing. Weekly or monthly checks are plenty, and a simple log or app makes the trend easy to see.

If you’re changing your weight on purpose, watch your BMI move toward the healthy range alongside other signs that matter more day to day, such as energy, sleep, fitness and how your clothes fit. If your BMI changes noticeably without trying, especially unexpected weight loss, mention it to your doctor.

Key terms

  • Body mass index (BMI): weight relative to height, in kg/m².
  • Healthy weight range: the weights that give a BMI of 18.5 to 24.9 at your height.
  • Waist circumference: a measure of abdominal fat, used alongside BMI.
  • BMI-for-age percentile: how children’s and teens’ BMI is interpreted, compared with others of the same age and sex.

Frequently asked questions

How is BMI calculated?

BMI is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in meters squared. In US units, it's 703 × weight in pounds ÷ height in inches squared. For example, 150 pounds at 5 feet 5 inches (65 inches) gives 703 × 150 ÷ 65² = 24.96, which rounds to 25.0.

What is a healthy BMI for adults?

The CDC and WHO define a healthy weight BMI for adults as 18.5 to 24.9. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25 to 29.9 is overweight, and 30 or above is obesity, which is further divided into class 1 (30 to 34.9), class 2 (35 to 39.9) and class 3 (40 and over).

Is BMI different for men and women?

The adult BMI formula and categories are the same for men and women. At the same BMI, though, women on average have more body fat than men, and BMI doesn't account for that. That's one reason BMI is used as a screening tool rather than a measure of body fat.

Does BMI work for athletes or older adults?

Less well. BMI can't tell muscle from fat, so very muscular people can have a high BMI without excess fat. Older adults often have less muscle, so their BMI can look normal even with more body fat. A waist measurement or a body fat estimate gives extra context.

Can children use this BMI calculator?

No. For children and teens aged 2 to 19, BMI is interpreted using age- and sex-specific percentiles on CDC growth charts, not the adult categories. Use the CDC's BMI calculator for children and teens, or ask your child's doctor.

What should I do if my BMI is high?

Treat it as a starting point for a conversation with a health professional, not a diagnosis. They can look at waist size, blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol and family history. If you want to lose weight, the calorie calculator can estimate a daily intake for a gradual, sustainable deficit.
