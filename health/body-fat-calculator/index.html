What this means

At 18% body fat, about 30.6 lb of your weight is fat and 139.3 lb is lean mass (muscle, bone, organs and water).

That’s in the “average” range on the American Council on Exercise chart for men.

The BMI method gives 22%. The two often differ by a few points; the tape method usually tracks change better because it measures you, not just your weight.

How to use this body fat calculator

Enter your height, weight and a few tape measurements, and the calculator estimates your body fat percentage with the US Navy method. It also shows the BMI-based estimate, your fat and lean mass, and your category on the ACE body fat chart.

  1. Units and sex. The Navy formula is different for men and women, and women also need a hip measurement.
  2. Age, height and weight. Age is used by the BMI method; weight is used to work out fat and lean mass.
  3. Neck and waist (and hips). Take each measurement twice and use the average; if the two readings differ by more than a quarter inch, take a third. Switching units converts the numbers you’ve entered.

Worked example: a woman at 5 feet 4 inches

A 34-year-old woman is 5 feet 4 inches (64 inches) tall and weighs 145 pounds. Her neck measures 13 inches, her waist 30 inches and her hips 38 inches.

  • Navy formula: 163.205 × log₁₀(30 + 38 − 13) − 97.684 × log₁₀(64) − 78.387 = 29.2% body fat
  • Fat mass: 145 × 29.2% ≈ 42 pounds; lean mass: about 103 pounds
  • ACE category: average (25% to 31% for women)
  • BMI method: BMI 24.9 gives 1.20 × 24.9 + 0.23 × 34 − 5.4 = 32.3%

For comparison, a 42-year-old man who is 5 feet 10 inches, weighs 185 pounds and has a 15.5-inch neck and 36-inch waist comes out at 20.4% body fat by the Navy method (about 38 pounds of fat), also in the average range. His BMI-based estimate is 25.3%.

The two methods differ by 3 to 5 points in both examples, which is typical. Pick one method and stick with it for tracking, because switching methods can look like a change in body fat when nothing has changed.

The formulas in plain English

The US Navy method estimates body density from circumference measurements, which reflect how much fat you carry around the middle relative to your frame, measured in inches:

  • Men: 86.010 × log₁₀(waist − neck) − 70.041 × log₁₀(height) + 36.76
  • Women: 163.205 × log₁₀(waist + hip − neck) − 97.684 × log₁₀(height) − 78.387

The BMI method, from a 1991 study by Deurenberg and colleagues, estimates body fat from BMI, age and sex: 1.20 × BMI + 0.23 × age − 10.8 × sex − 5.4, where sex is 1 for men and 0 for women.

Metric measurements are converted to inches with the exact factor (2.54 cm per inch) before applying the Navy formula. The Show the math panel shows your numbers in each formula.

How to take the measurements

The Navy method is only as good as the tape measurements, and an inch either way changes the result by a few percentage points. Use a flexible, non-stretch tape and follow the same routine each time:

  1. Height: barefoot, standing straight against a wall.
  2. Neck: just below the larynx (Adam’s apple), with the tape sloping slightly down toward the front. Keep your head level and don’t flare your neck.
  3. Waist: for men, horizontally at the level of the navel; for women, at the narrowest point of the abdomen, usually a little above the navel. Measure at the end of a normal breath out, without sucking in.
  4. Hips (women only): around the widest part of the buttocks, with feet together.

Take each measurement twice and use the average; if the two readings differ by more than a quarter inch, take a third. Measure in the morning before eating, and have someone else hold the tape if you can; it’s hard to keep a tape level around your own hips.

Healthy body fat ranges by age

Unlike the ACE categories, some research-based ranges rise with age. A widely cited study by Gallagher and colleagues (2000) linked healthy BMI ranges to body fat percentages measured by DEXA:

Age Women Men
20–39 21–32% 8–19%
40–59 23–33% 11–21%
60–79 24–35% 13–24%

These ranges are broad, and body fat that’s too low can be as much of a concern as too much, particularly for women, where very low levels can disrupt hormones and menstrual cycles.

Losing fat without losing muscle

When people diet, the scale counts every pound lost the same way, but your health and appearance depend on whether it’s fat or muscle. Body fat percentage is the way to tell the difference.

Take the woman in the example: 145 pounds at 29.2% body fat, about 42 pounds of fat and 103 pounds of lean mass. If she loses 10 pounds and all of it is fat, she ends up at about 24% body fat. If she loses 10 pounds but 4 of them are muscle, she’s at about 27%, with less strength and a lower metabolic rate for the same scale weight. The scale shows the same 135 pounds in both cases; only the body fat reading tells them apart.

Three habits tip the balance toward fat loss:

  • Strength training two or three times a week gives your muscles a reason to stay.
  • Enough protein, spread through the day, provides the building blocks to maintain muscle in a calorie deficit.
  • A moderate deficit rather than a crash diet. The bigger the deficit, the more muscle tends to be lost along with fat.

Why body fat estimates disagree

If you’ve used a smart scale, a gym’s handheld device and this calculator, you’ve probably seen three different numbers. That’s normal. Every method estimates body fat indirectly, from circumferences, electrical resistance, skin thickness or body density, and each has an error of several percentage points for an individual. Prediction formulas like the Navy method were developed on specific groups, such as military personnel, so they fit some body types better than others.

What matters is choosing one method and using it consistently. A reading that falls from 29% to 26% over three months on the same method is meaningful even if a different method would have said 27% to 24%.

Other ways to measure body fat

The tape method is free and repeatable, but it’s one of several options, each with trade-offs:

  • DEXA scan. A low-dose X-ray that maps fat, lean tissue and bone. It’s often treated as a reference method in research and is offered for a fee by some hospitals, universities and private clinics. Results still vary a little between machines, so repeat scans on the same machine when you can.
  • Bioelectrical impedance (BIA). Many bathroom scales and handheld devices send a tiny current through the body. They’re convenient, but readings swing with hydration, recent meals and exercise, so consistent timing matters even more than with a tape.
  • Skinfold calipers. A trained person pinches the skin at several sites. Accurate in skilled hands, much less so when self-administered.
  • Hydrostatic (underwater) weighing and air displacement (Bod Pod). Lab methods that estimate body density. Accurate, but rarely available outside universities and sports clinics.

Whatever method you choose, the most useful number is the change between measurements taken the same way. A drop in waist size with stable weight, for example, usually means you’re losing fat and keeping or gaining muscle.

Key terms

  • Body fat percentage: the share of your weight that is fat tissue.
  • Lean mass: everything that isn’t fat: muscle, bone, organs and water.
  • Essential fat: fat needed for normal body function; going below it is unhealthy.
  • DEXA scan: an X-ray method often used as a reference for body composition.
  • Visceral fat: fat stored around the organs in the abdomen; waist size is a rough indicator of it.

What to do with your result

Body fat gives a clearer picture than weight alone. Compare it with your BMI: if your BMI is high but your body fat is in the fitness range, much of your weight is likely muscle.

To lower body fat while keeping muscle, combine a moderate calorie deficit from the calorie calculator with enough protein from the macro calculator and regular strength training. The ideal weight calculator offers another reference point for a target weight.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is the US Navy body fat method?

For most adults it's typically within about 3 to 4 percentage points of laboratory methods such as underwater weighing or DEXA, according to validation studies, as long as the measurements are taken carefully. It's less accurate for very lean or very muscular people and when the tape is placed inconsistently. It works best for tracking change over time with the same technique.

How do I measure my neck, waist and hips?

Use a soft, non-stretch tape. Measure the neck just below the Adam's apple with the tape sloping slightly down at the front. Men measure the waist horizontally at the navel; women at the narrowest point of the torso. Women measure the hips at the widest part of the buttocks. Stand relaxed and measure after breathing out, without pulling the tape tight.

What is a healthy body fat percentage?

On the American Council on Exercise chart, the "fitness" range is 14% to 17% for men and 21% to 24% for women, and "average" is 18% to 24% for men and 25% to 31% for women. Essential fat, the minimum needed for health, is about 2% to 5% for men and 10% to 13% for women. Healthy ranges also rise somewhat with age.

Why are the Navy and BMI methods different?

The BMI method estimates body fat from weight, height, age and sex alone, so it can't see whether your weight is muscle or fat. The Navy method uses your actual waist and neck size, which reflect where you carry fat. For a muscular person, the BMI method usually overestimates body fat; for someone with little muscle, it may underestimate.

Is this the same formula the military uses?

It's the circumference formula developed for the US Navy in 1984, which became the basis for military tape tests. Individual services have since updated their procedures and standards; for example, the Army adopted a one-site waist tape test in 2023. Use the official procedure if you're preparing for a service assessment.

How often should I measure body fat?

Every 2 to 4 weeks is enough. Body fat changes slowly, and measurement error can hide small changes. Measure at the same time of day, before eating, with the same tape and technique, and focus on the trend across several readings.
