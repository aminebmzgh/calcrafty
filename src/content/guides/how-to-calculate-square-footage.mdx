## The basic method

Square footage is the number of 1 × 1 foot squares that fit in a space. To find it:

1. **Sketch the space.** Draw the outline of the room or area on paper. It doesn't need to be to scale.
2. **Split it into simple shapes.** Rectangles, triangles and circles (or parts of circles).
3. **Measure each shape** in feet, writing the numbers on your sketch.
4. **Work out each area** with the right formula.
5. **Add the areas,** or subtract a piece that's cut out of a larger shape.

The formulas you'll need:

| Shape | Area |
|---|---|
| Rectangle or square | length × width |
| Triangle | base × height ÷ 2 |
| Circle | π × radius² (radius = half the diameter) |
| Half circle | π × radius² ÷ 2 |
| Trapezoid | (side a + side b) ÷ 2 × height |

The [square footage calculator](/construction/square-footage-calculator/) does the rectangle, L-shape, circle and triangle cases for you, in feet, inches, yards or meters.

## Measuring in feet and inches

Tape measures give feet and inches, so convert before multiplying. Divide the inches by 12 to get a decimal of a foot: 6 inches is 0.5 ft, 9 inches is 0.75 ft, 4 inches is 0.333 ft.

**Example:** a room 13 ft 4 in by 11 ft 9 in is 13.333 × 11.75 = **156.67 sq ft**.

You can also work entirely in inches: 160 × 141 = 22,560 square inches, then divide by 144 (the square inches in a square foot) to get the same 156.67 sq ft. The mistake to avoid is treating inches as decimals: 13 ft 4 in is not 13.4 ft.

## L-shaped rooms

Split an L into two rectangles along one of the inside walls. There are two ways to do it, and both give the same answer as long as the pieces don't overlap.

**Example:** an L-shaped living area measures 25 ft by 12 ft along the long side, with a 10 × 8 ft section sticking out. The area is 25 × 12 + 10 × 8 = 300 + 80 = **380 sq ft**.

You can also use subtraction: measure the full rectangle that encloses the L, then subtract the missing corner. The same room fits in a 25 × 20 ft outline with a 15 × 8 ft corner missing: 500 − 120 = 380 sq ft.

## Angled corners and bay windows

**Angled corner:** treat the room as a full rectangle and subtract the triangle cut off by the angled wall. A 15 × 12 ft room with a corner cut at 3 ft along each wall loses a triangle of 3 × 3 ÷ 2 = 4.5 sq ft, leaving **175.5 sq ft**.

**Bay window:** most bays are trapezoids, with a wide side along the room and a narrower side at the window. Average the two parallel sides and multiply by the depth. A bay 8 ft wide at the wall, 4 ft wide at the front and 2 ft deep is (8 + 4) ÷ 2 × 2 = **12 sq ft**. Add it to the main room.

## Circles and curved areas

For a round patio or rug area, measure the diameter, halve it for the radius, and multiply π by the radius squared:

- A circle 10 ft across: π × 5² = **78.54 sq ft**.
- A half circle 10 ft across, such as a curved sunroom end: 78.54 ÷ 2 = **39.27 sq ft**.
- A quarter circle with a 6 ft radius, such as a corner garden bed: π × 6² ÷ 4 = **28.27 sq ft**.

For a gentle curve that isn't a true arc, divide the area into narrow strips, measure each strip's length and width, and add them. The more strips, the closer you get.

## Triangles and irregular polygons

For a triangle, you need the base and the height measured at a right angle to it. If you can only measure the three sides, Heron's formula works: with sides a, b and c, let s = (a + b + c) ÷ 2, and the area is √(s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)). A triangle with sides 13, 14 and 15 ft has s = 21 and an area of √(21 × 8 × 7 × 6) = **84 sq ft**.

Any polygon with straight sides can be split into triangles from one corner. Measure each triangle and add them up.

## A whole floor plan

For a home, measure room by room and keep a table:

| Room | Size | Area |
|---|---|---|
| Living room | 18 × 14 ft | 252 sq ft |
| Kitchen | 12 × 10 ft | 120 sq ft |
| Bedroom 1 | 12 × 11 ft | 132 sq ft |
| Bedroom 2 | 10 × 10 ft | 100 sq ft |
| Bathroom | 8 × 5 ft | 40 sq ft |
| Hallway | 12 × 3.5 ft | 42 sq ft |
| **Total** | | **686 sq ft** |

This interior total is always less than the figure measured from the outside, because it leaves out the thickness of the walls. A home listed at 750 to 800 sq ft could easily measure 686 sq ft inside. When you're buying flooring or comparing furniture layouts, the interior figure is the one you want.

## How homes are measured for sale

Appraisers and many builders in the U.S. follow the ANSI Z765 standard for the finished living area:

- Measure from the **outside of the exterior walls**.
- Count only **finished, heated space** that's part of the house.
- Count space only where the ceiling is **at least 7 feet** high, with rules for sloped ceilings and beams.
- List **below-grade space**, such as a basement, separately from above-grade space, even when it's finished.
- Leave out **garages, unfinished attics, porches and decks**.

Listings don't always follow the standard, so two homes advertised at the same square footage can differ. If price per square foot matters to your decision, ask how the figure was measured.

## Walls and ceilings

**Walls:** multiply the perimeter of the room by the wall height, then subtract openings. For a 15 × 12 ft room with 8 ft walls, the wall area is 2 × (15 + 12) × 8 = 432 sq ft. Subtract a standard door (about 21 sq ft) and two windows (about 15 sq ft each), leaving **381 sq ft**. The [paint calculator](/construction/paint-calculator/) turns that into gallons.

**Flat ceilings** have the same area as the floor. **Sloped ceilings** that follow the roof are larger: multiply the floor area by the pitch factor. For a 6-in-12 pitch, the factor is √(1 + (6 ÷ 12)²) = 1.118, so a 180 sq ft floor has about **201.25 sq ft** of ceiling. The same factor gives the roof area from the house footprint, as the [roofing calculator](/construction/roofing-calculator/) does.

## Stairs

For flooring on stairs, measure the treads and risers separately. Thirteen treads 3 ft wide and 10 in deep add up to 13 × 3 × (10 ÷ 12) = **32.5 sq ft** of tread. Add the risers if you're covering them too, plus extra for the nosing that wraps over each tread edge.

## Metric measurements

If you measure in meters, multiply the same way to get square meters, then convert: 1 m² = 10.7639 sq ft. A 5 × 4 m room is 20 m², or **215.28 sq ft**. To go the other way, multiply square feet by 0.0929. For converting individual lengths, the [length converter](/conversion/length-converter/) is exact.

## Adding a waste allowance

When buying material, add extra for cuts, mistakes and pattern matching:

- **Straight-laid flooring:** 5% to 10%.
- **Tile, or diagonal and herringbone patterns:** 10% to 15%.
- **Rooms with many corners or angles:** at the higher end.

For a 156.67 sq ft room with 10% extra, buy at least 172.34 sq ft. Then round up to whole boxes; the [flooring calculator](/construction/flooring-calculator/) does both steps. Keep a spare box after the job: later batches may not match exactly.

## Outdoor areas

Lawns, patios and decks use the same method on a bigger scale. Pace or tape the main dimensions, split the space into rectangles and curves, and leave out areas such as flower beds or the house footprint by subtracting them. A backyard 60 × 40 ft with a 12 × 10 ft patio has 2,400 − 120 = 2,280 sq ft of lawn for seed or sod. For gravel, mulch or soil, multiply the area by the depth in feet to get cubic feet, then divide by 27 for cubic yards.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing units,** such as multiplying feet by inches.
- **Counting overlaps twice** when splitting an L-shape.
- **Using a slanted side as a triangle's height.**
- **Measuring at the baseboard** instead of wall to wall at floor level, which slightly understates the area.
- **Comparing an interior total with a listing figure** that was measured from the outside.

## Tips for accurate measurements

- Use a 25 ft tape or a laser measure, and have someone hold the other end on long walls.
- Measure each wall at floor level, and measure long walls twice, at both ends.
- Round each measurement up to the next inch.
- Write every number on your sketch as you go, so you don't have to remeasure.
- Double-check the total against a rough estimate: a "12 by 12 room" should come out near 144 sq ft.

## Key terms

- **Square foot:** the area of a 1 × 1 ft square, 144 square inches.
- **Perimeter:** the total length around the edge of a shape.
- **Pitch factor:** the multiplier that turns a flat footprint into the area of a sloped surface.
- **Gross living area:** a home's finished, above-grade area, measured from the exterior walls.
- **Waste allowance:** extra material bought to cover cuts and errors.
