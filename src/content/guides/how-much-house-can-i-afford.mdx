**Short answer:** most lenders will let you spend up to about **28% of your gross monthly income** on a mortgage payment (including property tax and insurance) and up to **36% on all debts combined**. In practice, that puts the affordable home price at roughly **3 to 3.5 times your annual income** at today's rates, before accounting for other debts, local taxes and your down payment. The [mortgage affordability calculator](/finance/mortgage-affordability-calculator/) works out your exact number.

## How much house can I afford by income?

These figures assume a 30-year fixed loan at 7%, a down payment of 40% of annual income, 1% property tax, $1,800 a year of homeowners insurance, PMI when the down payment is under 20%, and $400 a month of other debt payments.

| Household income | Down payment | Maximum home price | Monthly payment |
|---|---|---|---|
| $60,000 | $24,000 | about $178,000 | $1,400 |
| $90,000 | $36,000 | about $276,000 | $2,100 |
| $120,000 | $48,000 | about $375,000 | $2,800 |
| $150,000 | $60,000 | about $473,000 | $3,500 |

In each case the 28% housing ratio is the limit. The monthly payment is exactly 28% of gross monthly income, and it includes principal, interest, property tax, insurance and PMI.

## The rules lenders use

Lenders look at two debt-to-income (DTI) ratios:

- **Front-end ratio:** your total housing payment divided by gross monthly income. The traditional maximum is 28%.
- **Back-end ratio:** housing plus all other monthly debt payments (car, student loans, card minimums) divided by gross monthly income. The traditional maximum is 36%; many programs allow 43% or more.

Your housing budget is the lower of the two limits. With $90,000 of income, 28% is $2,100 a month. If you also had a $900 car payment, the 36% rule would allow only $2,700 − $900 = $1,800 for housing, and the affordable price would fall from about $276,000 to about $239,000.

## What changes your budget most

**Interest rate.** A lower rate means more of each payment goes to the home instead of interest. For the $90,000 household:

| Rate | Maximum home price |
|---|---|
| 5.5% | about $310,000 |
| 6.5% | about $287,000 |
| 7.5% | about $267,000 |

Improving your credit score and comparing several lenders on the same day can be worth tens of thousands of dollars of buying power.

**Other debts.** Every dollar of monthly debt reduces the housing budget dollar for dollar once the 36% rule binds. Paying off a car loan before applying can raise your limit noticeably.

**Down payment.** A bigger down payment shrinks the loan and, at 20%, removes PMI. Plan it with the [savings goal calculator](/finance/savings-goal-calculator/).

**Location.** Property tax rates vary several-fold between states and counties, and insurance costs vary even more. A 2% property tax rate instead of 1% can cut the affordable price by around 10%.

## A better question: what payment fits your life?

The lender's maximum is based on gross income. Your real budget is based on take-home pay and everything else you spend money on. A practical approach:

1. Find your monthly take-home pay with the [paycheck calculator](/finance/paycheck-calculator/).
2. Subtract fixed costs (transport, childcare, insurance, debt payments) and savings goals (retirement, emergency fund).
3. Decide what's left for housing, including about 1% of the home's value each year for maintenance.
4. Use that payment in the [mortgage calculator](/finance/mortgage-calculator/) to see the price it supports.

If that number is lower than the lender's maximum, shop at your number. Being "house poor," with most of your income going to the mortgage, makes every other goal harder.

## Don't forget the cash you need

Beyond the down payment, budget for:

- **Closing costs:** typically 2% to 5% of the price for lender fees, title, appraisal and prepaid taxes and insurance.
- **Moving and setup:** movers, furniture, utility deposits.
- **An emergency fund:** a new home brings new repairs. Keeping several months of expenses in savings protects you from putting them on a credit card.

## Should you buy at all?

If you might move within a few years, the costs of buying and selling can outweigh the equity you build. The [rent vs. buy calculator](/finance/rent-vs-buy-calculator/) compares your net worth under each choice and shows the year buying breaks even.
