**Short answer:** to do a Punnett square, list the gametes each parent can produce, put one parent's gametes along the top of a grid and the other's down the side, and fill each box with the alleles from its row and column. Then count the boxes. For two heterozygous parents, **Aa × Aa**, the boxes are AA, Aa, Aa and aa: a **1 : 2 : 1** genotype ratio and, with A dominant, a **3 : 1** phenotype ratio. The [Punnett square calculator](/biology/punnett-square-calculator/) draws the square for up to three genes and checks your work.

## What a Punnett square shows

A Punnett square is a grid that shows every way the alleles from two parents can combine in their offspring. Each parent has two alleles for each gene, but passes on only one in each egg or sperm cell (gamete). The square lists the possible gametes from each parent and pairs them up, so every box is one equally likely combination.

It answers questions like: if two pea plants that both carry the gene for short height are crossed, what fraction of the offspring will be short? It works for any trait controlled by a single gene with simple dominance, and it can be extended to two or three genes at once.

## Step by step: a monohybrid cross

Let's cross two pea plants that are both heterozygous for height. T is the dominant allele for tall and t the recessive allele for short. Both parents are **Tt**.

### Step 1: write the genotypes

Parent 1: **Tt**. Parent 2: **Tt**.

### Step 2: list each parent's gametes

Each gamete gets one allele of the gene. A Tt parent makes two kinds of gamete, **T** and **t**, in equal numbers.

### Step 3: draw the grid

Draw a 2 × 2 grid. Write parent 1's gametes down the left side and parent 2's along the top.

### Step 4: fill in the boxes

Each box gets the allele from its row plus the allele from its column. Write the dominant (capital) allele first by convention.

| Gametes | T | t |
|---|---|---|
| **T** | TT | Tt |
| **t** | Tt | tt |

### Step 5: count the genotypes

- TT: 1 box
- Tt: 2 boxes
- tt: 1 box

The genotype ratio is **1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt**.

### Step 6: work out the phenotypes

TT and Tt both have at least one dominant T, so both are tall. Only tt is short. That's 3 tall boxes to 1 short: a **3 : 1** phenotype ratio, or **75% tall and 25% short**.

## Reading probabilities from the square

Every box is equally likely, so:

- In a 2 × 2 square, each box is **1/4 = 25%**.
- In a 4 × 4 square, each box is **1/16 = 6.25%**.

The chance of an outcome is the number of boxes with that outcome over the total. From Tt × Tt, the chance of a tt offspring is 1/4, and the chance of a tall offspring is 3/4.

These are probabilities for **each** offspring, not counts you'll see exactly. Four offspring from Tt × Tt won't necessarily include exactly one short plant; with hundreds, the proportions get close to 3 : 1. To work with these fractions, the [fraction calculator](/math/fraction-calculator/) and [percentage calculator](/math/percentage-calculator/) help.

## Step by step: a dihybrid cross

A dihybrid cross follows two genes at once. Suppose R (round seeds) is dominant over r (wrinkled), and Y (yellow seeds) is dominant over y (green). We'll cross two plants heterozygous for both: **RrYy × RrYy**.

### Finding the gametes with FOIL

Each gamete gets one allele of each gene. A quick way to list them is **FOIL**, the method used to multiply brackets in algebra:

- **First:** R and Y → **RY**
- **Outer:** R and y → **Ry**
- **Inner:** r and Y → **rY**
- **Last:** r and y → **ry**

So each parent makes four kinds of gamete, and the square is 4 × 4, with 16 boxes.

### Filling in the square

| Gametes | RY | Ry | rY | ry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RY** | RRYY | RRYy | RrYY | RrYy |
| **Ry** | RRYy | RRyy | RrYy | Rryy |
| **rY** | RrYY | RrYy | rrYY | rrYy |
| **ry** | RrYy | Rryy | rrYy | rryy |

### Counting phenotypes

Group the boxes by what the seeds look like:

- **Round and yellow** (at least one R and one Y): **9** boxes.
- **Round and green** (at least one R, yy): **3** boxes.
- **Wrinkled and yellow** (rr, at least one Y): **3** boxes.
- **Wrinkled and green** (rr yy): **1** box.

That's the classic **9 : 3 : 3 : 1** ratio, which Mendel found in his pea experiments.

### A shortcut: multiply the probabilities

Because the two genes are inherited independently, you can treat each one as its own monohybrid cross and multiply:

- Rr × Rr gives round 3/4 of the time, wrinkled 1/4.
- Yy × Yy gives yellow 3/4 of the time, green 1/4.
- Round and yellow: 3/4 × 3/4 = **9/16**.
- Wrinkled and green: 1/4 × 1/4 = **1/16**.

This is much faster for three or more genes, where the full square would have 64 or more boxes.

## Practice problems

Try each one, then check the answers below.

1. In guinea pigs, black fur (B) is dominant over brown (b). What fraction of the offspring of **Bb × Bb** will be brown?
2. A tall pea plant (Tt) is crossed with a short plant (tt). What are the genotype and phenotype ratios?
3. A plant **RrYy** is crossed with **rryy**. What is the phenotype ratio?
4. A plant **RRYy** is crossed with **rrYy**. What fraction of offspring will have round, yellow seeds?
5. Two parents are **TtRr** and **TtRr**. What fraction of offspring will show both recessive traits?

### Answers

1. **1/4 (25%) brown.** The square gives 1 BB : 2 Bb : 1 bb, and only bb is brown.
2. **Genotypes 1 Tt : 1 tt; phenotypes 1 tall : 1 short.** Half the offspring are Tt (tall) and half tt (short). This is a **test cross**: crossing with a homozygous recessive reveals the other parent's alleles.
3. **1 : 1 : 1 : 1.** The four gametes of RrYy each pair with ry, giving round yellow, round green, wrinkled yellow and wrinkled green in equal numbers.
4. **3/4 (75%).** Every offspring gets R from the RR parent, so all are round. For color, Yy × Yy gives yellow 3/4 of the time.
5. **1/16 (6.25%).** The chance of tt is 1/4 and of rr is 1/4, so 1/4 × 1/4 = 1/16.

## Working backward from the offspring

Exam questions often give you the offspring and ask for the parents' genotypes. The trick is to start with what you know for certain:

- **A recessive phenotype means a homozygous recessive genotype.** A short pea plant must be tt.
- **A dominant phenotype means at least one dominant allele.** A tall plant is T_, either TT or Tt.
- **Recessive offspring reveal hidden alleles.** If two tall parents have a short offspring, each parent must have passed on a t, so both are Tt.

Then check your answer by drawing the square. For example, if a cross produces roughly half tall and half short offspring, a Tt × tt cross fits; Tt × Tt would give about three-quarters tall. If all of many offspring are tall, at least one parent is probably TT.

## Drawing the square neatly

A few habits make squares easier to read, especially on tests:

- **Label the axes** with which parent is which.
- **Write gametes outside the grid,** not inside the boxes.
- **Keep the order of genes the same** in every box, such as R before Y.
- **Shade or mark boxes by phenotype** before counting, so none are missed.

## Tips for avoiding mistakes

- **Use one letter per gene,** capital for dominant and lowercase for recessive. Don't use different letters for the two alleles of the same gene.
- **Pick letters that look different in upper and lower case.** S/s, C/c and O/o are easy to confuse when handwritten.
- **List gametes correctly.** Each gamete has exactly one allele per gene: AB, not AaB.
- **Homozygous parents still get their gametes listed.** An RR parent makes only R gametes; you can write R twice or use a smaller grid.
- **Count phenotypes, not just genotypes,** when the question asks what offspring look like.

## When Punnett squares don't tell the whole story

The simple ratios assume complete dominance and genes that assort independently. Some traits break those assumptions:

- **Incomplete dominance:** the heterozygote is a blend, such as pink flowers from red × white, so the phenotype ratio matches the genotype ratio, 1 : 2 : 1.
- **Codominance:** both alleles show, as with AB blood type.
- **Sex-linked traits:** genes on the X chromosome need a square that tracks X and Y chromosomes separately.
- **Linked genes:** genes close together on a chromosome tend to be inherited together, distorting the 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 ratio.
- **Polygenic traits:** traits like human height depend on many genes and the environment.

## Key terms

- **Allele:** a version of a gene.
- **Genotype:** the alleles an individual has, such as Tt.
- **Phenotype:** the trait that shows, such as tall.
- **Homozygous:** two identical alleles (TT or tt).
- **Heterozygous:** two different alleles (Tt).
- **Gamete:** an egg or sperm cell carrying one allele per gene.
- **Test cross:** a cross with a homozygous recessive individual to reveal an unknown genotype.
- **Monohybrid cross:** a cross that follows a single gene.
- **Dihybrid cross:** a cross that follows two genes at the same time.
