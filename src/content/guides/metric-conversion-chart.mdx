**Short answer:** the key exact conversions are **1 inch = 2.54 cm**, **1 foot = 0.3048 m**, **1 mile = 1.609344 km**, **1 pound = 0.45359237 kg**, **1 U.S. gallon = 3.785411784 liters**, and **°F = °C × 9/5 + 32**. Everything else follows from these. The tables below give the most common conversions, and the [length converter](/conversion/length-converter/) converts any length exactly.

## How metric units work

The metric system, formally the International System of Units (SI), builds every measurement from a few base units: the **meter** for length, the **kilogram** for mass, the **liter** (a derived unit) for volume, and the **kelvin** or degree Celsius for temperature. Larger and smaller units are made with prefixes that are powers of ten:

| Prefix | Symbol | Factor | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| kilo | k | 1,000 | 1 km = 1,000 m |
| hecto | h | 100 | 1 hL = 100 L |
| deci | d | 0.1 | 1 dL = 0.1 L |
| centi | c | 0.01 | 1 cm = 0.01 m |
| milli | m | 0.001 | 1 mL = 0.001 L |
| micro | µ | 0.000001 | 1 µm = 0.000001 m |

Converting within the metric system is just a matter of moving the decimal point: 1.25 km is 1,250 m, and 750 mL is 0.75 L. That's its main advantage over U.S. customary units, where 12 inches make a foot, 3 feet a yard and 5,280 feet a mile.

## Length

| U.S. unit | Metric equivalent |
|---|---|
| 1 inch | 2.54 cm (exact) |
| 1 foot | 30.48 cm, 0.3048 m (exact) |
| 1 yard | 0.9144 m (exact) |
| 1 mile | 1.609344 km (exact) |

| Metric unit | U.S. equivalent |
|---|---|
| 1 centimeter | 0.3937 inch |
| 1 meter | 39.37 inches, 3.2808 feet |
| 1 kilometer | 0.621371 mile |

Because the inch is defined as exactly 2.54 cm, every length conversion is exact. Six feet is exactly 182.88 cm. A 5K race is about 3.11 miles and a 10K about 6.21 miles; the [running pace calculator](/sports/running-pace-calculator/) works out paces for them.

## Weight (mass)

| U.S. unit | Metric equivalent |
|---|---|
| 1 ounce | 28.349523125 g (exact) |
| 1 pound | 0.45359237 kg (exact), 453.59 g |
| 1 stone (14 lb) | 6.35 kg |
| 1 short ton (2,000 lb) | 907.18 kg |

| Metric unit | U.S. equivalent |
|---|---|
| 1 gram | 0.0353 ounce |
| 1 kilogram | 2.20462 pounds |
| 1 metric ton (tonne) | 2,204.62 pounds |

Example: a 150 lb person weighs 150 × 0.45359237 = **68.04 kg**; a 70 kg person weighs 70 ÷ 0.45359237 = **154.32 lb**.

Strictly, kilograms measure mass and pounds can measure either mass or force, but in everyday use both describe how heavy something is.

|---|---|
| 1 teaspoon | 4.93 mL |
| 1 tablespoon | 14.79 mL |
| 1 fluid ounce | 29.57 mL |
| 1 cup | 236.59 mL |
| 1 pint | 0.4732 L |
| 1 quart | 0.9464 L |

| Metric unit | U.S. equivalent |
|---|---|
| 1 milliliter | 0.0338 fluid ounce |
| 1 liter | 33.814 fluid ounces, 1.0567 quarts |

U.S. and British volumes differ. The **imperial gallon** used in the U.K. is exactly 4.54609 liters, about 1.2 U.S. gallons, and imperial pints and fluid ounces differ from U.S. ones too. Recipes add another twist: many countries use a **metric cup of 250 mL**, and U.S. nutrition labels use **240 mL**. When converting a recipe, check where it's from; the [recipe scaler](/food/recipe-scaler/) resizes any recipe once the units are settled.

## Temperature

Temperature scales don't just differ in size; they start at different points, so conversion needs a formula rather than a single factor:

**°F = °C × 9/5 + 32**

**°C = (°F − 32) × 5/9**

| Celsius | Fahrenheit | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| −40 °C | −40 °F | Where the scales meet |
| 0 °C | 32 °F | Water freezes |
| 20 °C | 68 °F | Room temperature |
| 37 °C | 98.6 °F | Normal body temperature (about) |
| 100 °C | 212 °F | Water boils at sea level |
| 180 °C | 356 °F | Moderate oven |
| 200 °C | 392 °F | Hot oven |

Common oven settings in Fahrenheit: 350 °F is about 177 °C, 400 °F about 204 °C, and 425 °F about 218 °C. Recipes often round these to 175, 200 and 220 °C.

A quick mental shortcut for weather: **double the Celsius and add 30**. 20 °C becomes about 70 °F (exact: 68 °F). It's close for everyday temperatures but less accurate far from 10 °C.

**Kelvin**, used in science, has the same size of degree as Celsius but starts at absolute zero: K = °C + 273.15.

## Area

| U.S. unit | Metric equivalent |
|---|---|
| 1 square inch | 6.4516 cm² (exact) |
| 1 square foot | 0.09290304 m² (exact) |
| 1 square yard | 0.836127 m² |
| 1 acre (43,560 sq ft) | 4,046.86 m² |
| 1 square mile (640 acres) | 2.59 km² |

| Metric unit | U.S. equivalent |
|---|---|
| 1 square meter | 10.7639 sq ft |
| 1 hectare (10,000 m²) | 2.471 acres |

Area factors are the **square** of length factors: since 1 foot is 0.3048 m, 1 square foot is 0.3048² = 0.09290304 m². Using the length factor for an area is a common mistake that gives answers far too large or small.

## Speed

| From | To | Factor |
|---|---|---|
| miles per hour | kilometers per hour | × 1.609344 |
| kilometers per hour | miles per hour | × 0.621371 |
| miles per hour | meters per second | × 0.44704 |
| knots | kilometers per hour | × 1.852 |

Example: 60 mph is **96.56 km/h**, 65 mph is **104.61 km/h**, and a 100 km/h speed limit is **62.14 mph**.

## Fuel economy

Fuel economy is measured differently, not just in different units. The U.S. uses **miles per gallon**, where a higher number is better. Much of the world uses **liters per 100 kilometers**, where a lower number is better, because it measures fuel used rather than distance traveled.

**L/100 km = 235.21 ÷ mpg**

The constant 235.21 comes from 100 × 3.785411784 ÷ 1.609344. A 25 mpg car uses about **9.41 L/100 km**; a 40 mpg car about **5.88 L/100 km**. The same formula works in reverse: mpg = 235.21 ÷ L/100 km. Some countries use kilometers per liter instead; 30 mpg is about 12.75 km/L.

Because the two measures are reciprocals, equal steps in mpg don't mean equal fuel savings. Going from 20 to 25 mpg saves 2.35 L per 100 km, while going from 35 to 40 mpg saves only about 0.84 L. That's one reason fuel-consumption measures are often preferred for comparing cars.

## Where you'll meet metric units in the U.S.

Even in the United States, metric units are everywhere:

- **Medicine:** doses are in milligrams and milliliters, and body weight for dosing is often in kilograms.
- **Nutrition labels:** protein, fat, carbohydrates and sodium are in grams and milligrams.
- **Science and engineering:** almost entirely metric.
- **Sports:** track events, swimming and most road races use meters and kilometers.
- **Drinks:** soda bottles come in liters, and wine in 750 mL bottles.
- **Cars and tools:** engine sizes are in liters, and many fasteners and wrenches are metric.

Knowing a handful of the conversions above makes it much easier to switch between the two systems as you need to.

## Quick rules of thumb

When an estimate is good enough:

- **Inches to centimeters:** × 2.5.
- **Feet to meters:** × 0.3.
- **Miles to kilometers:** × 1.6; kilometers to miles: × 0.6.
- **Pounds to kilograms:** ÷ 2.2, or halve and subtract 10%. 150 lb → 75 − 7.5 = 67.5 kg (exact: 68.04).
- **Kilograms to pounds:** × 2.2, or double and add 10%. 70 kg → 140 + 14 = 154 lb.
- **Liters and quarts:** about the same; a liter is a little more.
- **Celsius to Fahrenheit:** double and add 30.

For anything precise, such as medicine, construction or engineering, use the exact factors, and double-check the units before you start.

## Why the U.S. still uses customary units

The United States has recognized the metric system in law since the 1860s, and the Metric Conversion Act of 1975 declared it the preferred system for trade and commerce. But the switch was voluntary, and everyday life never fully changed: road signs, recipes, body weight and the weather are still mostly in customary units. Since 1959, however, those customary units have been defined in terms of metric ones, so the two systems are tied together exactly.

## Precision and rounding

How many digits you keep should match how precisely the original was measured. A board measured as "about 8 feet" doesn't become 2.4384 m of real precision; 2.4 m is honest. On the other hand, when an error multiplies, as in machining, medicine or construction, keep the exact factor until the end and round only the final answer. Rounding early, then converting again, can add errors that compound.

## Converting step by step

For any conversion, multiply by a factor written as a fraction that equals one, so the units cancel:

- **5 miles to meters:** 5 mi × (1,609.344 m ÷ 1 mi) = 8,046.72 m.
- **2.5 lb to grams:** 2.5 lb × (453.59237 g ÷ 1 lb) = 1,133.98 g.
- **3 cups to liters:** 3 cups × (0.2365882 L ÷ 1 cup) = 0.71 L.

If the units don't cancel to the ones you want, the fraction is upside down. Flip it and try again.

## Key terms

- **SI:** the International System of Units, the modern metric system.
- **U.S. customary units:** inches, pounds, gallons and the other units used in the United States.
- **Imperial units:** the British system, with a larger gallon and pint than the U.S.
- **Exact conversion:** a factor fixed by definition, such as 1 inch = 2.54 cm.
- **Conversion factor:** the number used to change a measurement from one unit to another.
- **Kelvin:** the SI unit of temperature, starting at absolute zero, with degrees the same size as Celsius.
- **Hectare:** 10,000 square meters, the metric unit for land area.
