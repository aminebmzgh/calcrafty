**Short answer:** in the United States, the most common guideline is **15% to 20% of the pre-tax bill** for table service at restaurants, with **20%** widely treated as standard for good service. Delivery drivers, bartenders, hairdressers and rideshare drivers are commonly tipped in a similar range. Tipping at counters and for takeout is optional. Norms vary by region and have shifted in recent years, so treat the figures below as typical ranges, not rules. The [tip calculator](/other/tip-calculator/) works out the tip and splits the bill.

## Why tipping matters in the U.S.

In much of the U.S., many restaurant servers and bartenders earn a lower base wage, with tips making up a large part of their income. Federal law allows employers to pay tipped employees a lower cash wage as long as tips bring them up to the full minimum wage, and some states require the full minimum wage before tips. Either way, tips are a significant part of pay for many service workers, which is why tipping customs are stronger in the U.S. than in many other countries.

## Restaurants and bars

| Situation | Typical tip |
|---|---|
| Sit-down restaurant, good service | 18% to 20% or more |
| Sit-down restaurant, adequate service | 15% |
| Buffet | around 10% |
| Bartender | $1 to $2 per drink, or 15% to 20% of the tab |
| Takeout | optional; small amount or about 10% for large orders |
| Coffee shop or counter service | optional; spare change or a dollar or two |
| Sommelier | often 15% to 20% of the wine, or included in the overall tip |

A **20% tip** is easy to work out: move the decimal point one place left to find 10%, then double it. On a $65 bill, 10% is $6.50 and 20% is $13.00.

### Large groups and service charges

Many restaurants add an **automatic gratuity**, often around 18% to 20%, for groups of six or more. Check the bill: if a gratuity or service charge is already included, you don't need to add a full tip on top, though you can add extra for exceptional service. Some restaurants now add a service charge for everyone; it's fair to ask whether it goes to the staff.

## Delivery

| Service | Typical tip |
|---|---|
| Restaurant delivery | 15% to 20%, with a minimum of a few dollars |
| Grocery delivery | 15% to 20% |
| Large or heavy deliveries | more, especially upstairs or in bad weather |
| Pizza delivery | 15% to 20%, or a few dollars at least |

App fees such as "service fee", "delivery fee" or "busy fee" often go to the platform, not the driver. The tip is usually the main part of what the driver earns from your order.

## Transportation

| Service | Typical tip |
|---|---|
| Taxi | 15% to 20% of the fare |
| Rideshare | 15% to 20%, added in the app |
| Airport shuttle driver | a few dollars, more for help with luggage |
| Valet parking | a few dollars when the car is returned |
| Skycap or porter | a dollar or two per bag |

## Personal care

| Service | Typical tip |
|---|---|
| Hairdresser or barber | 15% to 20% |
| Manicure or pedicure | 15% to 20% |
| Massage therapist (at a spa) | 15% to 20% |
| Shampoo assistant | a few dollars |

If the owner of a salon provides the service, tipping has traditionally been optional, but many people now tip owners the same as other stylists.

## Hotels and travel

| Service | Typical tip |
|---|---|
| Housekeeping | a few dollars per night, left daily |
| Bellhop | a dollar or two per bag |
| Concierge | optional; more for special arrangements |
| Room service | 15% to 20%, unless a service charge is included |
| Tour guide | varies; often a set amount per person per day |

Leaving housekeeping tips daily, rather than at the end, makes sure they go to the person who cleaned the room each day.

## Home services

| Service | Typical tip |
|---|---|
| Movers | often a set amount per mover, more for long or difficult moves |
| Furniture or appliance delivery | a modest amount per person for heavy items |
| Contractors and plumbers | generally not expected |
| House cleaner (regular) | often an extra amount at the holidays |

For movers, a common approach is a fixed amount per mover for a half day, and more for a full day or a move with many stairs. Offering water or a meal on a long, hot moving day is also appreciated.

## Holiday tipping

Many people give an extra tip at the end of the year to people who provide regular service: a house cleaner, babysitter, dog walker, hairdresser or building staff. A common guideline is the cost of one regular visit or service. For mail carriers and other public employees, rules limit what they may accept, so check before giving cash.

## Quick ways to work out a tip

- **10%:** move the decimal point one place left. $47.80 → $4.78.
- **20%:** double 10%. $47.80 → $9.56.
- **15%:** 10% plus half of it. $4.78 + $2.39 = $7.17.
- **Round first:** treat $47.80 as $48; 20% is $9.60.

On common bills:

| Bill | 15% | 18% | 20% |
|---|---|---|---|
| $20 | $3.00 | $3.60 | $4.00 |
| $50 | $7.50 | $9.00 | $10.00 |
| $100 | $15.00 | $18.00 | $20.00 |
| $150 | $22.50 | $27.00 | $30.00 |

Or use the [tip calculator](/other/tip-calculator/), which also splits the bill between friends and can round each share up to the dollar.

## Pre-tax or post-tax?

Etiquette guides generally say it's fine to tip on the amount **before tax**, since tax isn't part of the service. Many people tip on the total to keep it simple. The difference is small on most bills: 20% on a $60 meal is $12.00 before tax, or $13.02 on a total of $65.10.

When part of the bill was discounted, covered by a coupon or comped, it's considerate to tip on the **original** amount, since the service was the same.

## Tip screens at the counter

Card readers at coffee shops, bakeries, food trucks and even self-checkout kiosks now often suggest tips of 15%, 20% or 25%. These prompts don't change the etiquette: tipping for counter service has traditionally been optional. If someone made a custom drink, handled a large order or went out of their way, a tip is a nice gesture; otherwise "no tip" or a custom amount is perfectly acceptable. Read the screen carefully, since the preset buttons are sometimes calculated on the total after tax.

## Cash or card

Card tips are convenient and leave a record. Many workers appreciate cash because they receive it immediately rather than in a later paycheck. Either way works. If you're paying cash for a split bill, rounding each share up to the next dollar keeps things simple and adds a little to the tip.

## Splitting the bill and the tip

When friends share a check, the fairest approach depends on what everyone ordered. Splitting evenly is easiest when orders were similar. For a $142.60 bill split four ways with a 20% tip, the tip is $28.52 and the total $171.12, or $42.78 each; rounding up to $43 each gives a total of $172 and a tip of $29.40. If one person ordered much more, they can pay their own subtotal plus the same tip percentage.

## Budgeting for tips

Tips add up. A $15 delivery order with a $3 tip, three times a week, comes to about $468 a year in tips alone. When planning a dining budget, include tax and tip: a $50 meal is roughly $60 to $65 once a 20% tip and local sales tax are added.

## When service is poor

A tip reflects service, but it often makes up much of a worker's pay, and problems may not be the server's fault: a slow kitchen or understaffing are common causes. Many people still leave a reduced tip, such as 10% to 15%, for disappointing service and raise the problem with a manager. Leaving no tip is usually reserved for serious problems.

## Tipping outside the U.S.

Customs vary widely. In many European countries, service is included in prices and a small amount or rounding up is appreciated rather than expected. In Japan, tipping is generally not part of the culture. Some countries add service charges automatically. Look up local customs before you travel, and use the [percentage calculator](/math/percentage-calculator/) for other percentage questions along the way.

## For people who earn tips

Tips are taxable income. The IRS requires employees who receive $20 or more in tips in a month to report them to their employer. For tax years 2025 through 2028, eligible workers in occupations that customarily receive tips can deduct up to $25,000 of qualified tips a year from federal taxable income, with the deduction phasing out at higher incomes; Social Security and Medicare taxes still apply. The [paycheck calculator](/finance/paycheck-calculator/) helps estimate take-home pay.

## Key terms

- **Gratuity:** another word for a tip.
- **Automatic gratuity:** a tip added to the bill by the restaurant, often for large groups.
- **Service charge:** a fee added by the business, which may or may not go to staff.
- **Tipped employee:** a worker who customarily receives tips as part of their pay.
- **Tip credit:** the part of the minimum wage an employer may cover with an employee's tips, where state law allows it.
- **Pre-tax subtotal:** the cost of the food, drinks or service before sales tax is added.
