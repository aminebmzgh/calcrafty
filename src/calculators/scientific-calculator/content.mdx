## How to use this scientific calculator

Type an expression in the box or tap the keys, and the answer updates live. There's no equals button to press. Below the answer, the calculator shows how it read your expression, with every operator written out, so you can catch a misplaced parenthesis before it matters.

- **Keyboard:** type numbers and operators directly. Use `*` for ×, `/` for ÷ and `^` for powers. The `/` key won't open site search while you're typing.
- **Keypad:** tap buttons to insert functions at the cursor. ⌫ deletes, C clears.
- **Angles:** switch between degrees and radians for sin, cos, tan and their inverses.

Every calculation can be shared with **Copy link**, and your recent ones are saved privately in your browser's history.

**A practical example:** the hypotenuse of a right triangle with sides 7 and 24 is sqrt(7^2 + 24^2) = sqrt(625) = **25**.

**Compound growth:** $5,000 growing 6% a year for 12 years is 5000 × 1.06^12 ≈ **10,060.98**.

## Functions available

- **Arithmetic:** + − × ÷, parentheses, % (divides by 100), ! (factorial up to 170!)
- **Powers and roots:** ^, sqrt( ), cbrt( ), exp( )
- **Logarithms:** ln( ) natural, log( ) base 10, log2( ) base 2
- **Trigonometry:** sin, cos, tan and asin, acos, atan, in degrees or radians
- **Hyperbolic:** sinh, cosh, tanh
- **Rounding and sign:** abs( ), round( ), floor( ), ceil( )
- **Constants:** π (type pi), e

## How the calculator works

Your expression is broken into numbers, names and operators, then read by a parser that applies the standard precedence rules: parentheses, then postfix ! and %, then powers (right-associative), then negation, then multiplication and division (including implicit multiplication), then addition and subtraction. It never uses a general-purpose "evaluate code" function, so typing anything unusual simply produces a clear message.

Calculations use double-precision floating-point numbers (IEEE 754), the same standard used by spreadsheets and most programming languages, and results are shown to 12 significant digits. In degree mode, sine, cosine and tangent of whole multiples of common angles are cleaned to exact values, so sin(180°) shows 0 rather than a tiny leftover like 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁶. Undefined results, such as tan(90°), are reported rather than shown as huge numbers.

## Common mistakes

- **Negative bases with powers.** −3^2 is −9. If you mean "negative three, squared," type (−3)^2 = 9.
- **Degrees vs radians.** sin(30) is 0.5 in degrees but about −0.988 in radians. Check the toggle when trig answers look odd.
- **Missing parentheses with fractions.** 1/2π means (1/2) × π, about 1.571; type 1/(2π) if π belongs in the denominator.
- **Percent.** 50% is simply 0.5 here. For "what is 20% of 80" or percent change, the percentage calculator is clearer.

## Tips for getting reliable answers

Long expressions are where most mistakes happen, so a few habits help:

- **Add parentheses generously.** They never hurt, and they make your intent explicit. Write (a + b) / (c + d), not a + b / c + d.
- **Check the "Read as" line.** It shows exactly how the calculator understood your input, with every multiplication written out.
- **Watch the angle mode.** Trig answers that look strange are usually in the wrong mode. A quick test: sin(90) should be 1 in degrees.
- **Break long problems into steps.** Calculate a part, copy the result, then use it in the next expression. Your history keeps every step.
- **Sanity-check the size of the answer.** If 12% of $250 comes out as $3,000, a decimal point went astray.
- **Mind significant figures.** The calculator shows up to 12 digits, but your answer is only as precise as your least precise input. If you measured to two decimal places, round the result sensibly.

For repeated financial or statistical calculations, a dedicated calculator is usually faster and less error-prone than a long expression: percentage, compound interest and standard deviation each have their own tools on Calcrafty, linked below.

## Key terms

- **Order of operations:** the agreed sequence for evaluating an expression.
- **Radian:** the angle where the arc length equals the radius; 180° = π radians.
- **Natural logarithm (ln):** the logarithm with base e ≈ 2.71828.
- **Factorial (n!):** the product of all whole numbers from 1 to n.
- **Significant digits:** the digits that carry meaning in a number's precision.
