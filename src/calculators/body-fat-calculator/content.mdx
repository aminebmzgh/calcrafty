## How to use this body fat calculator

Enter your height, weight and a few tape measurements, and the calculator estimates your body fat percentage with the US Navy method. It also shows the BMI-based estimate, your fat and lean mass, and your category on the ACE body fat chart.

1. **Units and sex.** The Navy formula is different for men and women, and women also need a hip measurement.
2. **Age, height and weight.** Age is used by the BMI method; weight is used to work out fat and lean mass.
3. **Neck and waist (and hips).** Take each measurement twice and use the average. Switching units converts the numbers you've entered.

## Worked example: a woman at 5 feet 4 inches

A 34-year-old woman is 5 feet 4 inches (64 inches) tall and weighs 145 pounds. Her neck measures 13 inches, her waist 30 inches and her hips 38 inches.

- **Navy formula:** 163.205 × log₁₀(30 + 38 − 13) − 97.684 × log₁₀(64) − 78.387 = **29.2% body fat**
- **Fat mass:** 145 × 29.2% ≈ **42 pounds**; **lean mass:** about **103 pounds**
- **ACE category:** average (25% to 31% for women)
- **BMI method:** BMI 24.9 gives 1.20 × 24.9 + 0.23 × 34 − 5.4 = **32.3%**

For comparison, a 42-year-old man who is 5 feet 10 inches, weighs 185 pounds and has a 15.5-inch neck and 36-inch waist comes out at **20.4%** body fat by the Navy method (about 38 pounds of fat), also in the average range. His BMI-based estimate is 25.3%.

The two methods differ by 3 to 5 points in both examples, which is typical. Pick one method and stick with it for tracking, because switching methods can look like a change in body fat when nothing has changed.

## The formulas in plain English

The US Navy method estimates body density from circumference measurements, which reflect how much fat you carry around the middle relative to your frame, measured in inches:

- **Men:** 86.010 × log₁₀(waist − neck) − 70.041 × log₁₀(height) + 36.76
- **Women:** 163.205 × log₁₀(waist + hip − neck) − 97.684 × log₁₀(height) − 78.387

The BMI method, from a 1991 study by Deurenberg and colleagues, estimates body fat from BMI, age and sex: 1.20 × BMI + 0.23 × age − 10.8 × sex − 5.4, where sex is 1 for men and 0 for women.

Metric measurements are converted to inches with the exact factor (2.54 cm per inch) before applying the Navy formula. The **Show the math** panel shows your numbers in each formula.

## ACE body fat categories

| Category | Women | Men |
|---|---|---|
| Essential fat | 10–13% | 2–5% |
| Athletes | 14–20% | 6–13% |
| Fitness | 21–24% | 14–17% |
| Average | 25–31% | 18–24% |
| Obese | 32% and over | 25% and over |

These ranges, from the American Council on Exercise, are a general guide for adults. They don't change with age, but body fat tends to rise as people get older, so a slightly higher reading at 60 than at 30 is normal.

## Other ways to measure body fat

The tape method is free and repeatable, but it's one of several options, each with trade-offs:

- **DEXA scan.** A low-dose X-ray that maps fat, lean tissue and bone. It's often treated as a reference method in research and is offered for a fee by some hospitals, universities and private clinics. Results still vary a little between machines, so repeat scans on the same machine when you can.
- **Bioelectrical impedance (BIA).** Many bathroom scales and handheld devices send a tiny current through the body. They're convenient, but readings swing with hydration, recent meals and exercise, so consistent timing matters even more than with a tape.
- **Skinfold calipers.** A trained person pinches the skin at several sites. Accurate in skilled hands, much less so when self-administered.
- **Hydrostatic (underwater) weighing and air displacement (Bod Pod).** Lab methods that estimate body density. Accurate, but rarely available outside universities and sports clinics.

Whatever method you choose, the most useful number is the change between measurements taken the same way. A drop in waist size with stable weight, for example, usually means you're losing fat and keeping or gaining muscle.

## Key terms

- **Body fat percentage:** the share of your weight that is fat tissue.
- **Lean mass:** everything that isn't fat: muscle, bone, organs and water.
- **Essential fat:** fat needed for normal body function; going below it is unhealthy.
- **DEXA scan:** an X-ray method often used as a reference for body composition.
- **Visceral fat:** fat stored around the organs in the abdomen; waist size is a rough indicator of it.

## What to do with your result

Body fat gives a clearer picture than weight alone. Compare it with your [BMI](/health/bmi-calculator/): if your BMI is high but your body fat is in the fitness range, much of your weight is likely muscle.

To lower body fat while keeping muscle, combine a moderate calorie deficit from the [calorie calculator](/health/calorie-calculator/) with enough protein from the [macro calculator](/health/macro-calculator/) and regular strength training. The [ideal weight calculator](/health/ideal-weight-calculator/) offers another reference point for a target weight.
