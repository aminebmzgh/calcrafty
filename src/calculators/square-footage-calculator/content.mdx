## How to use this square footage calculator

1. **Pick the shape.** Rectangle covers most rooms. L-shape adds a second rectangle. Circle and triangle handle round patios, bay areas and gable ends.
2. **Pick the unit.** Enter measurements in the unit you used, whether feet, inches, yards, meters or centimeters. Use decimals for partial units: 12 feet 6 inches is 12.5 feet.
3. **Enter the measurements.** Only the fields the shape needs are shown.
4. **More options.** Multiply by a number of identical areas (four bedrooms the same size, for example), add a percentage for waste, or enter a price per square foot for a cost estimate.

The result shows the area in square feet, with square meters, square yards and acres. The table below lists every unit, including square inches.

## Area formulas

| Shape | Formula | Example | Area |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rectangle or square | length × width | 15 ft × 12 ft | 180 sq ft |
| L-shape | (length₁ × width₁) + (length₂ × width₂) | (20 × 12) + (10 × 8) ft | 320 sq ft |
| Circle | π × (diameter ÷ 2)² | 10 ft across | 78.54 sq ft |
| Triangle | base × height ÷ 2 | 10 ft base, 6 ft high | 30 sq ft |

The height of a triangle is measured at a right angle to the base, not along a sloping side. For a circle, measure the diameter straight across through the center; half of it is the radius.

## Worked example: a 15 × 12 ft bedroom

Multiply the length by the width: 15 × 12 = **180 square feet**. In other units:

- **Square yards:** 180 ÷ 9 = 20 sq yd.
- **Square meters:** 180 × 0.09290304 = 16.72 m².
- **Square inches:** 180 × 144 = 25,920 sq in.

If you're buying flooring at $4.50 per square foot and add 10% for waste, you need 198 sq ft, which costs **$891.00** before installation. The [flooring calculator](/construction/flooring-calculator/) goes further, rounding up to full boxes and adding underlayment.

## Measuring feet and inches

Tape measures show feet and inches, but the math needs one unit. Convert inches to a decimal of a foot by dividing by 12:

| Inches | Decimal feet |
|---|---|
| 1 | 0.083 |
| 3 | 0.25 |
| 4 | 0.333 |
| 6 | 0.5 |
| 8 | 0.667 |
| 9 | 0.75 |

A room 13 ft 4 in by 11 ft 9 in is 13.333 × 11.75 = 156.67 sq ft. Alternatively, choose **Inches** as the unit and enter 160 × 141 inches; the calculator converts for you.

## Measuring a room accurately

- **Measure wall to wall at floor level**, where flooring will go, not at the baseboards or trim.
- **Measure in two places** on long walls. Rooms are rarely perfectly square; use the longer measurement when buying material.
- **Round up to the next inch.** A little extra is better than coming up short.
- **Include closets** if the flooring continues into them, and measure them as separate rectangles.
- **Leave out fixed cabinets and islands** for flooring, but include them if you're estimating heating or room size.
- **Sketch the room.** Draw the outline, note each measurement and divide odd shapes into rectangles before you start.

## Irregular rooms

Any floor plan with straight walls can be split into rectangles and triangles. For a room with an angled corner, measure the full rectangle and subtract the missing triangle. For a bay window, treat the bay as a trapezoid: average the two parallel sides, then multiply by the depth.

Round features such as a semicircular sunroom are half a circle: work out the circle's area and divide by 2. The guide [how to calculate square footage](/guides/how-to-calculate-square-footage/) walks through these cases with diagrams in words and examples.

## Converting between units

| From | To | Multiply by |
|---|---|---|
| Square feet | Square meters | 0.09290304 |
| Square meters | Square feet | 10.7639 |
| Square feet | Square yards | 1 ÷ 9 (0.1111) |
| Square yards | Square feet | 9 |
| Square inches | Square feet | 1 ÷ 144 |
| Square feet | Acres | 1 ÷ 43,560 |

These factors are exact apart from rounding, because the foot is defined as exactly 0.3048 meters. For converting lengths before you multiply, see the [length converter](/conversion/length-converter/).

## How much material to buy

Most materials are sold by area, with a waste allowance for cuts:

| Material | Sold by | Usual extra |
|---|---|---|
| Laminate, vinyl plank, hardwood | Square feet per box | 5% to 10% |
| Tile | Square feet per box | 10% to 15% (more for diagonal layouts) |
| Carpet | Square yards or feet, in 12 ft wide rolls | 10% or more, depending on seams |
| Sod | Square feet per piece or pallet | 5% to 10% |
| Paint | Square feet of wall per gallon | 1 extra quart for touch-ups |

Walls are a different area: for paint, multiply the room's perimeter by the wall height and subtract doors and windows, as the [paint calculator](/construction/paint-calculator/) does.

## Square footage of a house

The square footage in a home listing is the finished living area, and it's measured differently from a single room. The usual U.S. standard (ANSI Z765) measures from the outside of exterior walls, counts only finished, heated space with a ceiling of at least 7 feet, and lists basements separately from above-grade space. Garages, unfinished attics and open porches aren't included. That's why your own room-by-room total can be 10% to 15% lower than the listed figure: interior measurements leave out the thickness of walls.

When comparing homes by price per square foot, make sure both figures use the same method. Appraisers follow the standard, but listings sometimes don't.

## Price per square foot

Price per square foot is the price divided by the area, and it's the quickest way to compare homes, rents or renovation quotes of different sizes:

| Item | Price | Area | Price per sq ft |
|---|---|---|---|
| House A | $420,000 | 2,100 sq ft | $200.00 |
| House B | $365,000 | 1,650 sq ft | $221.21 |
| Apartment rent | $1,800 a month | 900 sq ft | $2.00 a month |

House B costs less in total but more per square foot. Neither number tells the whole story, since location, condition and lot size matter too, but price per square foot shows when a smaller home is priced high for its size. For contractor quotes, check what's included: "per square foot installed" often covers labor, while a materials price doesn't.

## Room size rules of thumb

Building codes set minimums for habitable rooms. Under the International Residential Code, which most U.S. states base their codes on, a habitable room must be at least 70 square feet and at least 7 feet across in any direction, with exceptions for kitchens. Local codes can differ, so check with your building department before finishing a basement or attic.

Common sizes for comparison: a small bedroom is often around 100 to 120 square feet (10 × 10 to 10 × 12 ft), a primary bedroom 200 square feet or more, and a one-car garage about 240 to 300 square feet. If you're planning furniture, the calculator's equivalent square in the explanation (for example, 180 sq ft is like a square 13.4 feet on each side) helps picture the space.

## Heating, cooling and other uses

Square footage feeds many other estimates:

- **Air conditioners and heaters.** A rough starting point for a room air conditioner is about 20 BTU per square foot, adjusted for sun, ceiling height and climate. A 180 sq ft room suggests about 3,600 BTU, so a 5,000 BTU unit, the smallest common size, is enough. Whole-house systems should be sized by a professional using a load calculation.
- **Lighting.** Lighting guides often give lumens per square foot by room type, such as more for kitchens and less for bedrooms.
- **Cleaning and painting quotes.** Many services price by square foot, so an accurate figure helps you check a quote.
- **Property tax and insurance.** Assessors and insurers use living area to estimate value and rebuilding cost; check that their figure matches your home.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing units.** Multiplying feet by inches gives a meaningless number. Convert everything to one unit first, or choose the unit in the calculator.
- **Using inches as decimals.** 12 feet 6 inches is 12.5 feet, not 12.6.
- **Counting an overlap twice.** When splitting an L-shaped room, make sure the two rectangles meet at a line and don't share a corner area.
- **Using the slanted side of a triangle as its height.** The height is the straight-up distance from the base.
- **Forgetting waste.** Buying exactly the floor area leaves nothing for cuts, and a later box may not match the color batch.

## Larger areas: lots and land

For yards and lots, the same formulas apply, and acres become useful. A lot 100 feet by 150 feet is 15,000 square feet, or 0.34 acres. A quarter acre is 10,890 square feet. Outside the U.S., land is usually measured in hectares: one hectare is 10,000 square meters, about 2.471 acres or 107,639 square feet. For gravel, mulch or soil, multiply the area by the depth to get volume, then use the [gravel calculator](/construction/gravel-calculator/) or the [concrete calculator](/construction/concrete-calculator/) for a slab.

## Key terms

- **Square foot (sq ft):** the area of a square 1 foot on each side, 144 square inches.
- **Square yard (sq yd):** 9 square feet.
- **Square meter (m²):** about 10.76 square feet.
- **Acre:** 43,560 square feet.
- **Waste allowance:** extra material bought to cover cuts, pattern matching and mistakes.
- **Gross living area:** the finished, above-grade living space of a home, measured from the exterior walls.
