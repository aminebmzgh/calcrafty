## How to use this days between dates calculator

Pick two dates and the calculator shows how many days lie between them, how many are business days, and the same span in weeks and months. It updates as you change either date.

1. **Start and end date.** Choose any dates; if the end is earlier than the start, they're swapped automatically.
2. **Count the end date too.** Leave unticked to count the days from the start up to, but not including, the end date (the usual way to count "days until"). Tick it to include both dates.
3. **Exclude US federal holidays.** Business days always skip weekends; this option also skips federal holidays that fall on weekdays, using their observed dates.

## Worked examples

**Countdown to Christmas.** From Monday, November 2, 2026 to Friday, December 25, 2026:

- **53 days**, or 7 weeks and 4 days, or 1 month and 23 days
- **14** of those days fall on weekends
- **2** weekday federal holidays are excluded: Veterans Day (Wednesday, Nov 11) and Thanksgiving (Thursday, Nov 26)
- That leaves **37 business days**

**A project schedule.** A project running from Monday, March 2 to Tuesday, June 30, 2026, counting both days, covers 121 calendar days. Taking out 34 weekend days, Memorial Day (May 25) and Juneteenth (Friday, June 19) leaves **85 working days** to plan around.

**A full year.** Counting January 1 through December 31, 2026 inclusive gives 365 days, 261 weekdays and 250 federal working days.

## How the count works in plain English

- **Calendar days:** the difference between the two dates, counting every day, including February 29 in leap years. With **Count the end date too**, one day is added.
- **Weeks:** days ÷ 7, with the remainder in days.
- **Months:** whole calendar months from the start date, then the leftover days. Adding a month to the 31st lands on the last day of shorter months.
- **Business days:** every counted day from Monday to Friday, minus weekday federal holidays when that option is on.

Federal holidays are calculated for every year in the range from the rules in 5 U.S.C. 6103: fixed dates such as July 4 and December 25, and "nth weekday" holidays such as Thanksgiving on the fourth Thursday of November. When a fixed-date holiday falls on a Saturday it's observed on Friday, and on a Sunday it's observed on Monday. For example, July 4, 2026 is a Saturday, so the holiday is observed on Friday, July 3.

## Dates 30, 60, 90 and 180 days out

Many deadlines are set as a number of days after an event. Starting from January 1, 2027 (not counting the start day):

| Days later | Date |
|---|---|
| 30 | January 31, 2027 |
| 60 | March 2, 2027 |
| 90 | April 1, 2027 |
| 180 | June 30, 2027 |
| 365 | January 1, 2028 |

Because months have different lengths, "90 days" and "3 months" are not the same: three months after January 1 is April 1 here only by coincidence, while 90 days after March 1 is May 30 but three months after March 1 is June 1. When a contract or rule says "days," count days; when it says "months," count calendar months.

## How many days are in each month and year

| Month | Days |
|---|---|
| January, March, May, July, August, October, December | 31 |
| April, June, September, November | 30 |
| February | 28, or 29 in a leap year |

An old memory trick uses your knuckles: make two fists side by side and count the months across them. Every month that lands on a knuckle has 31 days; every month in a dip between knuckles has 30, except February.

A common year has 365 days and a leap year 366. Leap years are the years divisible by 4, except century years that aren't divisible by 400, so 2000 was a leap year, 2100 won't be, and the next leap years are 2028 and 2032. A common year is 52 weeks and 1 day long, which is why a date falls one weekday later each year, or two days later after February 29.

## Bank holidays vs federal holidays

Banks usually close on federal holidays, so transfers, checks and loan payments due on a holiday are generally processed on the next business day. There's one notable difference: when a holiday falls on a Saturday, the federal government gives employees the preceding Friday off, but the Federal Reserve, which runs much of the banking system's payment network, stays open on that Friday. In 2027, for example, federal offices are closed on Friday, June 18 for Juneteenth, but many banks are open and payments still move. If a payment date matters, check your bank's own holiday calendar as well as this count.

## Week numbers

Many businesses plan by week number. Under the international ISO 8601 standard, weeks start on Monday, and week 1 is the week that contains the year's first Thursday. Most years have 52 ISO weeks, but a year that starts on a Thursday, or a leap year that starts on a Wednesday, has 53. 2026 began on a Thursday, so it has 53 ISO weeks; 2027 has 52. US calendars often number weeks differently, starting on Sunday, so check which system a schedule uses.

## Counting days in a spreadsheet

In Excel or Google Sheets, subtracting one date cell from another gives the number of days between them (=B1−A1). For business days, **NETWORKDAYS(start, end, holidays)** counts weekdays and lets you supply a list of holiday dates to skip. It counts both the start and end dates, so it matches this calculator with **Count the end date too** switched on. For a different weekend pattern, such as Friday and Saturday, NETWORKDAYS.INTL lets you choose which days count as the weekend.

## Common uses for counting days

People count days for very different reasons, and each has its own convention about the end date and business days:

- **Deadlines and notice periods.** Contracts, leases and legal notices often specify calendar days or business days. "Within 30 days" usually counts calendar days starting the day after the event; check the wording, as some agreements define their own rules.
- **Travel and hotel stays.** Hotels charge by night, which is the number of days between check-in and check-out without counting the last day. A trip from Friday to Sunday is 2 nights but 3 days of travel.
- **Projects and staffing.** Business days, minus holidays, tell you how many working days a team actually has.
- **Shipping and banking.** Delivery estimates and transfers usually count business days; banks also close on most federal holidays.
- **Countdowns.** The days until a birthday, wedding or holiday, where the event day itself isn't counted.
- **Pregnancy and medical intervals.** These are usually counted in completed weeks and days from a start date.

When the result has to match an official count, compare it with the rule's exact definition: whether the first and last days count, and which holidays apply. The calculator's two options cover the most common variations.

## Key terms

- **Business day:** a weekday that isn't a holiday.
- **Observed holiday:** the weekday on which a weekend holiday is taken off.
- **Inclusive count:** counting both the start and end dates.
- **Leap year:** a year with 366 days; February has 29 days.
