## How to use this paint calculator

1. **Room size.** Enter the length and width of the room and the height of the walls, in feet. For a room with a sloping or vaulted ceiling, use the average wall height, or calculate the sloping walls separately.
2. **Doors and windows.** Enter how many there are. Their area is subtracted, since you won't paint over them.
3. **Coats.** Two for most jobs; one for a fresh coat of the same color.
4. **Ceiling.** Tick the box to include it.
5. **More options.** Set the coverage per gallon from your can (most say 350 to 400 sq ft), and change the size of a door or window if yours are larger than standard.

The result tells you exactly what to buy, in whole gallons plus quarts, along with the wall area, the ceiling area if included, and how much paint will be left over for touch-ups.

## The formula

**Wall area = 2 × (length + width) × height**

**Area to paint = wall area − (doors × 21 sq ft) − (windows × 15 sq ft) + ceiling (length × width)**

**Gallons = area to paint × coats ÷ coverage per gallon**

The calculator then rounds up to whole gallons plus quarts for the remainder. If the remainder would need three or more quarts, it buys another gallon instead, because a gallon usually costs about as much as three quarts and covers more.

A standard interior door is about 3 × 7 ft, or 21 sq ft, and a typical window about 3 × 5 ft, or 15 sq ft. Large patio doors or picture windows are bigger, so adjust the sizes under More options.

## Worked example: a 12 × 14 ft bedroom

The room has 8-foot walls, one door and two windows, and gets two coats of paint that covers 350 sq ft per gallon.

- **Wall area:** 2 × (14 + 12) × 8 = **416 sq ft**.
- **Doors and windows:** 21 + 2 × 15 = **51 sq ft**.
- **Area to paint:** 416 − 51 = **365 sq ft**.
- **Gallons:** 365 × 2 ÷ 350 = **2.09 gallons**.
- **To buy:** **2 gallons + 1 quart**, leaving about 0.16 gallons for touch-ups.

Painting the ceiling as well adds 14 × 12 = 168 sq ft per coat, for 533 sq ft in all and 3.05 gallons: **3 gallons + 1 quart**. Ceilings are usually painted with a separate flat ceiling paint, so in practice you'd buy 2 gallons + 1 quart of wall paint and 1 gallon of ceiling paint.

## Paint needed for common rooms

Two coats at 350 sq ft per gallon, with 21 sq ft doors and 15 sq ft windows:

| Room | Size and walls | Openings | Area to paint | Gallons needed | To buy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small bathroom | 5 × 8 ft, 8 ft walls | 1 door, 1 window | 172 sq ft | 0.98 | 1 gallon |
| Small bedroom | 10 × 10 ft, 8 ft walls | 1 door, 1 window | 284 sq ft | 1.62 | 2 gallons |
| Bedroom | 12 × 12 ft, 8 ft walls | 1 door, 2 windows | 333 sq ft | 1.90 | 2 gallons |
| Bedroom | 12 × 14 ft, 8 ft walls | 1 door, 2 windows | 365 sq ft | 2.09 | 2 gallons + 1 quart |
| Living room | 16 × 20 ft, 9 ft walls | 2 doors, 3 windows | 561 sq ft | 3.21 | 3 gallons + 1 quart |

Bathrooms often need less paint than people expect, but mildew-resistant bathroom paint and the time spent cutting in around fixtures make them slower jobs.

## One accent wall

To paint just one wall, enter **half** the wall's length as the room length and 0 as the width, because the room formula counts opposite walls in pairs. Then set the doors and windows on that wall. For a 14 ft wall 8 ft high, enter 7 and 0: the calculator finds 14 × 8 = 112 sq ft. Two coats take 112 × 2 ÷ 350 = 0.64 gallons, or about 2½ quarts. Since three quarts usually cost about the same as a gallon, **one gallon** is the better buy, with plenty left for touch-ups.

## Rooms that aren't rectangles

- **L-shaped rooms:** add up the length of every wall to get the perimeter, then multiply by the height. Enter half the perimeter as the length and 0 as the width to get the same result.
- **Vaulted or sloped ceilings:** the gable-end walls are partly triangles. Calculate the rectangular part as usual and add half the base × the height of each triangle.
- **Stairwells:** tall walls in stairwells can be twice the height of a normal room. Measure them separately; they often need extension poles or scaffolding.
- **Open-plan spaces:** decide where one color stops and the next starts, usually at an inside corner, and calculate each area separately.

## How coverage changes the amount

Coverage is the single biggest assumption. For the same 12 × 14 ft room with two coats:

| Coverage per gallon | Gallons needed | To buy |
|---|---|---|
| 250 sq ft (textured or porous walls) | 2.92 | 3 gallons |
| 350 sq ft | 2.09 | 2 gallons + 1 quart |
| 400 sq ft (smooth, primed walls) | 1.83 | 2 gallons |

What reduces coverage:

- **Texture.** Orange-peel, knockdown and popcorn finishes have more surface area.
- **New drywall and bare plaster.** They absorb paint; prime them first.
- **Big color changes.** Covering a dark or bright color with a light one may need an extra coat, or a tinted primer.
- **Application method.** Rollers with a thick nap hold more paint; sprayers can waste 20% or more in overspray.

## One coat or two?

Most professionals apply two coats. The first coat seals and evens out the surface; the second gives a uniform color and sheen. One coat can be enough when:

- You're repainting the **same color** over a wall in good condition.
- You're using a **paint-and-primer** product over a similar color, and the label says one coat covers.

Plan on two coats, plus primer, when you're covering dark colors, stains, patches, new drywall or glossy surfaces.

## Primer

Primer seals porous surfaces, blocks stains and helps paint stick to glossy finishes. It typically covers less than paint, often around 200 to 300 sq ft per gallon, so use a lower coverage figure when calculating primer. To estimate it, set the calculator to one coat and the coverage on the primer can.

Use a primer when painting:

- New drywall or bare plaster.
- Patched areas, so they don't show through as dull spots.
- Over water stains, smoke damage or knots in wood.
- Over glossy paint, after a light sanding.
- A light color over a dark one.

## Trim, doors and ceilings

The calculator covers walls and, optionally, the ceiling. Trim and doors are usually painted with a harder-wearing semi-gloss or gloss paint:

- **Baseboards and casings:** a quart is often enough for one room's trim.
- **Doors:** a standard door is about 21 sq ft per side; two coats on both sides of one door use roughly a quarter of a gallon.
- **Ceilings:** a flat ceiling paint hides imperfections and reduces glare.

## Choosing a sheen

| Sheen | Where it works | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Flat or matte | Ceilings, low-traffic rooms | Hides flaws, harder to clean |
| Eggshell | Living rooms, bedrooms | Slight sheen, easier to wipe |
| Satin | Kitchens, hallways, kids' rooms | More durable and washable |
| Semi-gloss | Trim, doors, bathrooms | Resists moisture, shows flaws |
| Gloss | Cabinets, railings | Very durable, highly reflective |

Sheen doesn't change the amount of paint much, but higher sheens show roller marks and surface flaws more, so careful preparation matters more.

## Exterior walls

The same formula works for the outside of a house: use the perimeter of the house and the height from the foundation to the eaves, then subtract doors, windows and garage doors. Exterior paint often covers less than interior paint on rough siding, stucco or brick, so check the can and lower the coverage figure. Add the triangular gable ends separately.

## Older homes and lead paint

Homes built before 1978 may contain lead-based paint. Sanding or scraping it releases toxic dust, especially dangerous for children and pregnant women. In the United States, contractors working on these homes must follow the EPA's Lead Renovation, Repair and Painting rules. If you're doing it yourself, test before sanding and follow lead-safe work practices.

## Tips for buying paint

- **Buy all the paint at once,** and mix the cans together in a bucket (called boxing) so the color is consistent across walls.
- **Keep the leftover paint** sealed and labeled with the room and date for touch-ups.
- **Round up, not down.** A second trip to the store mid-job often means a slightly different batch.
- **Sample first.** Paint a large swatch on two walls and look at it in daylight and at night before committing.

## Key terms

- **Coverage:** the area one gallon of paint covers in one coat.
- **Cutting in:** painting the edges along ceilings, corners and trim with a brush before rolling.
- **Primer:** a base coat that seals the surface and helps paint adhere.
- **Sheen:** how glossy a paint's finish is, from flat to gloss.
- **Boxing:** mixing several cans of paint together for an even color.
