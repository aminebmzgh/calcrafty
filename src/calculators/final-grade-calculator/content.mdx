## How to use this final grade calculator

You need three numbers from your gradebook and syllabus: your current grade, the grade you want, and how much the final counts. The calculator shows the score you need and how every possible final score would affect your course grade.

1. **Current grade.** Your grade in the class right now, before the final, as a percentage.
2. **Grade you want.** For example, 90 for an A or 80 for a B on a standard scale.
3. **Final exam weight.** The percentage of your course grade that the final is worth, from the syllabus.
4. **More options.** Enter a score you think you'll get to see the course grade it produces.

The result also lists the score you need for an A (90), B (80), C (70) and D (60), and a table of course grades for final scores from 50% to 100%.

## Worked example: holding on to a B+ or reaching an A−

Jordan has an 82.5% in a class where the final is worth 30%. They want to finish with at least an 85%.

- **Already earned:** 82.5 × (1 − 0.30) = **57.75** points of the course grade are locked in.
- **Score needed for 85%:** (85 − 57.75) ÷ 0.30 = **90.83%** on the final.
- **For a B (80%):** (80 − 57.75) ÷ 0.30 = **74.17%**
- **For an A (90%):** 107.5%, out of reach without extra credit.
- **Range:** a zero on the final gives 57.75%; a perfect score gives 87.75%.

If Jordan expects about 88% on the final, the course grade would be 57.75 + 88 × 0.30 = **84.15%**, just short of 85. That tells them where the extra study hours matter.

## The formula in plain English

Your course grade is a weighted average of everything before the final and the final itself:

**Course grade = current grade × (1 − final weight) + final score × final weight**

Rearranging for the score you need:

**Needed final score = (target − current grade × (1 − final weight)) ÷ final weight**

Weights are written as decimals in the formula: 30% becomes 0.30. The **Show the math** panel walks through both formulas with your numbers.

## Common situations

**The final replaces your lowest test.** Some instructors let a strong final replace a weak midterm. In that case, recalculate your current grade without the dropped test and use the final's combined weight.

**The final is a percentage of points, not a fixed weight.** If the final is 200 points in a 1,000-point course, its weight is 200 ÷ 1,000 = 20%.

**Curved grades.** If your class is curved, the letter cutoffs may be lower than 90/80/70/60. Enter the target percentage your instructor uses.

**Pass/fail or minimum final score rules.** Some courses require a minimum score on the final to pass, regardless of your average. Check the syllabus; the calculator can't know these rules.

**Weighted categories.** If homework is 20%, quizzes 20%, tests 30% and the final 30%, your current grade is the weighted average of the first three categories, scaled to the 70% they represent. Many online gradebooks show this as your current grade already.

## How to work out your current grade

If your gradebook doesn't show a current percentage:

- **Points-based courses:** add up the points you've earned and divide by the points possible so far. 412 out of 480 is 85.8%.
- **Weighted categories:** for each category, find your average, multiply it by the category weight, add the results, then divide by the total weight of the categories completed so far. For example, with homework (weight 20%) at 95% and tests (weight 40%) at 78%: (95 × 0.2 + 78 × 0.4) ÷ 0.6 = 83.7%.

The [percentage calculator](/math/percentage-calculator/) helps with the individual steps.

## Study strategy: where the points are

Once you know the score you need, you can plan your study time:

- **If the target is comfortably reachable,** review steadily and focus on not losing easy points: read every question carefully and check your answers.
- **If you need a high score,** prioritize the topics worth the most points and the ones you're weakest on. Past exams and practice problems from the instructor are usually the best guide to what's tested.
- **If the target is out of reach,** reset to the highest realistic goal. Raising a 79 to an 80 may still change your letter grade, and a strong final can matter for your GPA and for recommendation letters.
- **If your grade is secure,** use the time to strengthen other courses where the final makes more difference.

Sleep, a realistic schedule and practicing under timed conditions consistently do far more for exam scores than any amount of last-minute cramming.

## More than one assessment left?

If a project and a final are both still to come, treat everything remaining as one block. Add up the weights of everything that's left, and enter that as the "final" weight. The result is the average you need across all remaining work.

For example, with a 74% so far on work worth 60% of the course, and a project and final together worth the other 40%, you need (80 − 74 × 0.6) ÷ 0.4 = **89%** on average across both to finish with an 80%. If the project usually goes better than exams, you can split the effort accordingly: a 95% on a project worth 15% leaves a smaller score needed on the final.

## Why one exam can matter so much

A final's weight sets how much it can move your grade. On a 40% final, every 10 points on the exam changes your course grade by 4 points; on a 15% final, by only 1.5 points. That's why a heavily weighted final is both a risk and an opportunity: one bad day can pull down a strong semester, and one great exam can rescue a weak one.

It also explains a common surprise. If you're sitting at a 92% and the final is worth 50%, scoring 80% on it drops you to 86%, from an A to a B. Knowing the numbers in advance helps you decide whether the final deserves extra preparation, even when your grade looks safe.

## Grading scales other than 90/80/70/60

The letter grade table uses the most common US scale, but many schools differ. Some use plus and minus cutoffs, such as 93 for an A and 90 for an A−; others use a 7-point scale, with an A from 93 and a D from 70; some colleges grade on a curve, setting cutoffs after seeing everyone's results. Whatever the scale, the formula is the same: enter the exact percentage you need as the target, and the calculator gives the score required. If you're aiming for a plus or minus grade, check the syllabus for the precise cutoff, since even a fraction of a single point can decide the final letter.

## A final-exam checklist

- Confirm the exam's weight, date, time and allowed materials from the syllabus.
- Calculate the score you need now, so you know whether you're defending a grade or chasing one.
- Gather past exams, practice problems and review sheets.
- Plan study sessions over several days rather than one long night.
- Check whether there's any extra credit or a dropped-score policy.

## Key terms

- **Weighted average:** an average where each part counts by its share of the total.
- **Final exam weight:** the percentage of the course grade the final represents.
- **Current grade:** your weighted average before the final.
- **Letter grade cutoff:** the minimum percentage for a letter grade, commonly 90 for an A, 80 for a B, 70 for a C and 60 for a D.
- **Extra credit:** optional work that can add points beyond the normal maximum.
