faq:
  - q: How is a car payment calculated?
    a: First the amount financed is worked out as the price plus sales tax and fees, minus your down payment and the net value of your trade-in. Then the standard amortization formula, M = P × r(1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1), gives the fixed monthly payment, where r is the APR divided by 12 and n is the number of months.
  - q: Does a trade-in reduce sales tax?
    a: In most states, yes. You pay sales tax only on the price minus the trade-in value. On a $28,400 car with a $6,000 trade-in at 6.25%, that's $1,400 in tax instead of $1,775. A few states, including California, tax the full price regardless of a trade-in; untick the option under More options if that applies to you.
  - q: What happens if I owe more on my trade-in than it's worth?
    a: That gap is called negative equity, and dealers usually add it to your new loan. You then pay interest on the old car's debt as well as the new one, and you'll likely owe more than the new car is worth for longer. If you can, pay down the old loan or pay the difference in cash.
  - q: Is a 72- or 84-month car loan a good idea?
    a: Longer loans lower the payment but raise the total cost and often come with higher rates. On $23,550 at 7%, an 84-month loan costs $355.43 a month but $6,306.46 in interest, versus $466.32 and $4,429.12 over 60 months. Because cars lose value quickly, long loans also leave you owing more than the car is worth for much of the term.
  - q: How much should I put down on a car?
    a: Many lenders and consumer guides suggest about 20% down on a new car and 10% on a used one. A bigger down payment shrinks the loan, cuts interest and lowers the risk of owing more than the car is worth. Any amount helps, though, and the calculator shows the exact effect.
  - q: Should I get pre-approved for an auto loan?
    a: Getting a pre-approval from a bank or credit union before visiting the dealer gives you a rate to compare against dealer financing and keeps the conversation on the car's price, not just the monthly payment. The CFPB recommends shopping for financing the same way you shop for the car itself.

## How to use this auto loan calculator

Enter the price, down payment, rate and term to see your monthly payment immediately. Then add trade-in, tax and fees to get the number the dealer will actually quote.

1. **Vehicle price.** The negotiated price before tax, after any rebates.
2. **Down payment and rate.** The APR starts at the Federal Reserve's latest average for 60-month new-car loans at commercial banks. Used-car loans and lower credit scores usually carry higher rates.
3. **Loan term.** Tap a term from 36 to 84 months.
4. **Trade-in.** Enter what the dealer offers for your current car, and the payoff amount if you still have a loan on it.
5. **Sales tax and fees.** Use your combined state and local rate, plus title, registration and documentation fees.

## Worked example: a used SUV with a trade-in

A buyer agrees on a $28,400 used SUV, puts $2,500 down and trades in a car worth $6,000 that still has $1,800 owed on it. Sales tax is 6.25% and fees come to $450. They're approved at 7% for 60 months.

- **Sales tax:** ($28,400 − $6,000) × 6.25% = **$1,400**
- **Net trade-in:** $6,000 − $1,800 = $4,200
- **Amount financed:** $28,400 + $1,400 + $450 − $2,500 − $4,200 = **$23,550**
- **Monthly payment:** **$466.32**
- **Total interest:** $4,429.12, so the SUV costs $30,479.12 including the down payment

Here's how the term changes the same $23,550 loan:

| Term | Monthly payment | Total interest |
|---|---|---|
| 36 months | $727.16 | $2,627.58 |
| 48 months | $563.93 | $3,518.87 |
| 60 months | $466.32 | $4,429.12 |
| 72 months | $401.50 | $5,358.36 |
| 84 months | $355.43 | $6,306.46 |

Stretching from 60 to 84 months saves about $111 a month but adds $1,877 in interest.

## The car payment formula in plain English

There are two steps: work out how much you're borrowing, then turn it into a fixed monthly payment.

**Amount financed = price + sales tax + fees − down payment − (trade-in value − amount owed)**

**Payment = P × r(1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1)**, where P is the amount financed, r is the APR ÷ 12 and n is the number of months.

Each payment covers that month's interest (balance × r) and the rest reduces the balance. If you get 0% dealer financing, the payment is simply the amount financed divided by the number of months. The **Show the math** panel above walks through both steps with your numbers.

## Key terms

- **Amount financed:** what you actually borrow after tax, fees, down payment and trade-in.
- **APR:** the yearly cost of the loan including required finance charges.
- **Negative equity (being "upside down"):** owing more on a car than it's worth.
- **Out-the-door price:** the total you'd pay in cash, including tax and fees.
- **Pre-approval:** a conditional loan offer from a lender before you shop.

## What to do with your result

Negotiate the out-the-door price first and the financing second; a low monthly payment can hide a long term or a high price. Check the payment against your take-home pay with the [paycheck calculator](/finance/paycheck-calculator/), and plan a bigger down payment with the [savings goal calculator](/finance/savings-goal-calculator/).
