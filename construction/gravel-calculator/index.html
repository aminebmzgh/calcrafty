What this means

Covering 200 sq ft 3 inches deep takes 1.85 cubic yards; with 10% extra for compaction, order 2.04 cubic yards, about 2.85 tons at 1.4 tons per cubic yard.

Bulk delivery from a landscape or aggregate supplier is usually far cheaper than bags at this size. Suppliers sell by the ton or by the cubic yard, so ask which and convert with the figures above.

How to use this gravel calculator

  1. Length and width. Measure the area in feet. For a curved or irregular area, see the tips below.
  2. Depth. Enter how deep you want the gravel, in inches, after it’s spread and compacted.
  3. Extra for compaction. Loose gravel settles when it’s compacted and as it’s driven or walked on. 10% is a common allowance; use more for a driveway base that will be compacted with a plate compactor.
  4. More options. Enter your supplier’s weight per cubic yard (the default is 1.4 tons) and the size of the bags if you’re buying them.

The result shows the volume in cubic yards and cubic feet, the weight in tons and pounds, and the number of bags.

The formula

Cubic feet = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in) ÷ 12 × (1 + extra %)

Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27

Tons = cubic yards × tons per cubic yard

Bags = cubic feet ÷ bag size, rounded up

Weights here are U.S. short tons of 2,000 lb.

Worked example: a 20 × 10 ft patio area

Gravel 3 inches deep with 10% extra for compaction, at 1.4 tons per cubic yard:

  • Area: 20 × 10 = 200 sq ft.
  • Volume: 200 × 3 ÷ 12 = 50 cubic feet, or 1.85 cubic yards before the allowance.
  • With 10% extra: 55 cubic feet, or 2.04 cubic yards.
  • Weight: 2.04 × 1.4 = 2.85 tons, about 5,704 lb.
  • Bags: 55 ÷ 0.5 = 110 bags of 0.5 cubic feet.

A pickup truck can’t safely carry nearly 3 tons in one load, and 110 bags is a lot of handling, so this job suits a bulk delivery.

How much one ton covers

At 1.4 tons per cubic yard, a ton of gravel is about 19.3 cubic feet. Spread evenly, it covers:

Depth Area per ton
2 inches about 116 sq ft
3 inches about 77 sq ft
4 inches about 58 sq ft
6 inches about 39 sq ft

These figures are before compaction, so the area you can actually cover will be a little smaller.

Weight per cubic yard

Gravel is sold by the ton or by the cubic yard, so the conversion matters. Weight depends on:

  • The type of stone. Granite, limestone and river rock have different densities.
  • The size and grading. Mixed sizes, such as crusher run or road base, pack more tightly and weigh more per yard than single-size stone like pea gravel.
  • Moisture. Wet gravel weighs more than dry.

Typical planning figures run from about 1.4 to 1.7 tons per cubic yard. The calculator uses 1.4 by default; at 1.6, the patio example would be 3.26 tons rather than 2.85. Ask your supplier for the weight of their product and enter it under More options. If they sell by the yard, you don’t need the weight at all, except to check that your vehicle or trailer can carry it.

Choosing the right gravel

Material Typical use
Crushed stone, ¾ inch to 1½ inch Driveway and patio base layers, drainage
Crusher run or road base Compacted base for driveways, pavers and slabs
Pea gravel Paths, play areas, decorative beds
Fine crushed stone or stone dust Path surfaces, leveling under pavers
River rock Decorative beds, drainage swales

Angular crushed stone locks together when compacted, which makes it stable for driveways and bases. Round stones such as pea gravel and river rock roll underfoot and shift under tires, so they’re better for decoration and light foot traffic, ideally with an edging to keep them in place.

Building a gravel driveway

A durable gravel driveway is usually built in layers:

  1. Excavate and compact the soil, shaping it so water drains to the sides.
  2. Lay a geotextile fabric on soft or clay soils to stop the stone sinking into the ground.
  3. Base layer: larger crushed stone, compacted in lifts of a few inches at a time.
  4. Top layer: smaller crushed stone or crusher run, compacted to a firm surface.

Calculate each layer separately with its own depth, and add them together for the total order. Over time, gravel driveways need topping up as stone is pushed aside or sinks; a thin top-up layer every few years is normal.

Gravel under a shed

A shed is often set on a gravel pad that extends about a foot beyond the shed on every side, so rainwater drains away from the walls. For a 10 × 12 ft shed, that means a 12 × 14 ft pad. At 4 inches deep with 10% extra for compaction, it takes 61.6 cubic feet: 2.28 cubic yards, or about 3.19 tons at 1.4 tons per cubic yard. Contain the pad with treated timber or pavers so the stone stays put, and compact it in two layers.

Gravel for a French drain

A French drain is a gravel-filled trench, usually with a perforated pipe, that carries water away from a wet area or a foundation. For a trench 50 ft long, 1 ft wide and 18 inches deep, enter 50 for the length, 1 for the width and 18 for the depth, with no compaction allowance: that’s 75 cubic feet, or 2.78 cubic yards, about 3.89 tons. The pipe takes up a little of that space, so you’ll use slightly less. Drainage gravel should be clean, washed stone with no fines, so that water can flow through it; ask your supplier for drainage stone rather than crusher run.

Bulk delivery or bags?

Bags of about 0.5 cubic feet are convenient for small jobs, such as topping up a path, filling around pavers or decorating a planter. The patio example would need 110 of them.

Bulk delivery from a landscape or aggregate yard is far cheaper per ton for anything more than about a cubic yard. When you order:

  • Give the volume or weight from this calculator and the product name.
  • Ask about the minimum order and the delivery fee.
  • Plan where the truck will dump it, ideally close to the job on a tarp or on a hard surface, and away from septic systems and soft lawns that a heavy truck could damage.

Measuring irregular areas

  • Split the area into rectangles, calculate each one and add them up.
  • For a circle, the area is 3.14 × radius². Enter that area as the length and 1 as the width.
  • For a curved path, measure its length along the middle and multiply by the average width.
  • Check the depth after excavation in several places; uneven ground often needs more gravel in low spots.

Metric conversions

The calculator also shows the volume in cubic meters, using the exact factor 1 cubic foot = 0.028316846592 cubic meters. One cubic yard is about 0.765 cubic meters, so the patio example’s 2.04 cubic yards is about 1.56 cubic meters. A metric tonne is 1,000 kg, about 2,205 lb, which is roughly 10% heavier than a U.S. short ton.

Keeping gravel in place

  • Install edging of steel, plastic, timber or stone along paths and beds, so gravel doesn’t spread into the lawn.
  • Use landscape fabric under decorative gravel to slow weeds, but not under driveway base layers unless it’s a geotextile rated for the load.
  • Rake and top up each year or two; paths and driveways gradually lose depth as stone is kicked aside or pressed into the soil.
  • Keep it well drained. Puddles soften the base underneath and lead to potholes, so crown driveways slightly and keep side ditches clear.

Related projects

Gravel is often the base for other work. A concrete slab usually sits on about 4 inches of compacted gravel; the concrete calculator works out the concrete on top. If you are building a shed on a gravel pad, the roofing calculator works out the shingles for its roof, and the paint calculator the paint for its walls.

Key terms

  • Cubic yard: 27 cubic feet, the unit most bulk gravel is sold in.
  • Short ton: 2,000 lb, the ton used for gravel in the United States.
  • Compaction: pressing gravel down so it settles and locks together.
  • Crusher run: a mix of crushed stone and stone dust that compacts into a firm base.
  • Geotextile fabric: a permeable fabric laid under gravel to separate it from the soil.
  • Fines: the dust and small particles in crushed stone that help it compact but block drainage.

Frequently asked questions

How much gravel do I need?

Multiply length × width in feet by the depth in inches ÷ 12 to get cubic feet, then divide by 27 for cubic yards. A 20 × 10 ft area 3 inches deep is 50 cubic feet, or 1.85 cubic yards; with 10% extra for compaction, order about 2.04 cubic yards, roughly 2.85 tons.

How many tons of gravel are in a cubic yard?

Most gravel and crushed stone weighs about 1.4 to 1.7 tons per cubic yard, depending on the stone, its size and how wet it is. 1.4 tons is a common planning figure; ask your supplier for the figure for their product.

How much area does a ton of gravel cover?

At 1.4 tons per cubic yard, a ton is about 19.3 cubic feet. That covers roughly 116 sq ft at 2 inches deep, 77 sq ft at 3 inches or 58 sq ft at 4 inches.

How deep should gravel be for a driveway?

A gravel driveway is usually built in layers totaling 4 to 6 inches or more over compacted soil: a base of larger crushed stone, then a top layer of smaller stone. Soft or clay soils may need a deeper base or a geotextile fabric underneath.

How deep should gravel be for a path or patio?

About 2 to 3 inches of pea gravel or fine crushed stone for a walking path, over a compacted base. Under pavers or a concrete slab, a compacted base of about 4 inches is common.

How many bags of gravel make a cubic yard?

A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet, so it takes 54 bags of 0.5 cubic feet each. For anything more than about a cubic yard, bulk delivery is usually cheaper than bags.
